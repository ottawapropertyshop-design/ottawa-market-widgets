Ottawa's estate-lot village

Greely grew up as a farm-service centre on the Bank Street corridor — the old Ottawa–Prescott road, today Ottawa Road 31 — after Osgoode Township's first settler families arrived in 1827. The name is younger than the settlement: the post office was renamed Greely in 1886, and the welcome signs still carry the village's stagecoach emblem and an est. 1834 date from the corridor era. For a century it stayed a quiet crossroads village. The transformation began in 1990, when local developer Dan Anderson founded Sunset Lakes Developments in the village and started building estate-lot communities around spring-fed lakes — the first residents moved in in 1993, and the formula repeated community after community: Cedarlakes, Woodstream, South Village, Shadow Ridge and more, alongside the Emerald Links golf-course community. Greely joined the City of Ottawa in the 2001 amalgamation with its village character intact, and it is still growing on the same idea.

Getting around from Greely

Greely runs on cars, and it is honest about it. Bank Street carries the village north into the city — downtown is roughly a 30-minute drive and the airport 15 to 20 minutes — while Old Prescott Road and Meadow Drive run through the village core, Stagecoach Road crosses the east side, and Mitch Owens Road runs west to Manotick's larger retail. The Bank Street plaza covers the weekly list inside the village: grocery, pharmacy, medical and the everyday errands, with the Greely Community Centre and the library branch on Meadow Drive as the public square.

Schools around Greely

Schools are the organising spine for most Greely families, and the village's own setup looks like this:

  • In the village: Greely Elementary School (OCDSB, JK–6) stands at 7066 Parkway Road — the English-program elementary school in the village itself, within walking distance of the Parkway Road subdivisions.

  • French Immersion and secondary: Castor Valley Elementary School (OCDSB, JK–8) on Grey's Creek Road is the designated Early French Immersion school for the Greely and Metcalfe area, and public secondary runs to Osgoode Township High School in Metcalfe, Grades 9–12.

  • Catholic and French boards: St. Mary School (OCSB, JK–6) sits on Bank Street at the village's northern edge, feeding St. Mark Catholic High School (Grades 7–12) in Manotick; no French-language school sits in Greely itself, so French-board families work through the French boards' own locators.

Catchments turn on the exact street here, so check yours on each board's school-locator tool before you buy. My Greely guide points you to the same locators.

Parks, play and daily life

The Greely Community Centre at 1448 Meadow Drive is the village's living room: gymnasium drop-in sports, registered programs, hall rentals, Greely Players theatre productions, the Winter Carnival and Canada Day — and the Ottawa Public Library's Greely branch shares the building. The lakes do the recreation work: the estate communities wrap around their own spring-fed lakes with beaches, docks and trails through their owners associations, and the 27 holes at Emerald Links anchor the golf. Daily life runs on space itself — big lots, quiet streets, and working farmland at the village edges.

Something interesting about Greely

The village is most likely named for a man who never saw it: General Adolphus Washington Greely, the American Arctic explorer whose expeditions of 1881–84 were world-famous when the post office was renamed in 1886 — the Geographical Names Board's own file records the reasoning. An Ottawa village carrying an Arctic explorer's name, earned by newspaper fame rather than by any visit, is a very 19th-century piece of branding — and the name has now been on the map for well over a century.

Questions buyers ask about Greely

Is Greely on city water and sewer, or wells and septic?
Wells and septic. The village grew without municipal water or sewer pipes — even the estate communities run on private wells and septic systems, with the lakes doing the stormwater work — so a well and septic inspection, and servicing feasibility on any building lot, is the standing homework on a Greely purchase.
