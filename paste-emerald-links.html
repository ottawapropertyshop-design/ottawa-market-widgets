Emerald Links — a Patterson family course, and the community it built

Emerald Links starts with the Patterson family, who came to Canada from Ireland in 1884 and, in 1890, bought a 30-acre farm on Hunt Club Road west of Bank Street. Gib Patterson — born March 5, 1938 — grew up in that farming family (his brother Ford went on to run Patterson Berry Farm) and turned the family’s land knowledge into golf: Airport Golfland opened on July 1, 1971, and Innes Road Golfland followed on July 1, 1978.

Emerald Links itself opened in July 1990 — 18 holes in Greely, with the first estate homes already taking shape around it on the Phase 1 streets registered in 1988. The family kept building: Cloverdale Links in 1996 and 1998, then, on July 1, 2003, Emerald Links’ south nine opened along with the additional one-acre building lots — the course and the community growing in the same motion, which is how Emerald Links has always worked. Anderson Links followed on July 2, 2007, its own south nine opening May 12, 2012.

The club today — Emerald Links Golf & Country Club, 6357 Emerald Links Drive — runs 27 holes in three nines (West, South and East), semi-private, with a driving range, a golf academy and an events calendar that makes the clubhouse the community’s social centre. Martin Patterson is the club’s general manager. On the development side, the Pattersons’ Castor Creek Inc. is the developer of record for the community’s phases, and Patterson Homes Inc. — the family’s custom-building arm, led by Mark Patterson and based right in the community on Emerald Links Drive — builds custom homes here to this day.

The three phases of Emerald Links around the course — Phase 1 north and west, Phase 2 in the middle, Phase 3 south, between Mitch Owens Road and the rural land above Fox Valley Road.

The three phases, one by one

Phase 1 — the founding streets (Plans 4M-655/656, 1988). Emerald Links Drive and Tomkins Farm Crescent, wrapped around the original course, with the clubhouse inside the phase at 6357 Emerald Links Drive. These are the community’s established estate homes — the mature landscaping, the original fairway lots, and the shortest walk to the first tee. Resales here are events: owners stay, and when a Phase 1 home lists, the whole village hears about it.

Phase 2 — Pebblewoods Drive (Plan 4M-1267, registered June 6, 2005). A single street of 31 estate lots on 44.4 hectares, built around the pond that gives the street its name-sake setting. Phase 2 homes sit between the Phase 1 fairways and the Phase 3 lands — close enough to the club to use it daily, on lots that feel a full step larger than Phase 1’s.

Phase 3 — the custom-home estates (Plan 4M-1660). South of Pebblewoods Drive: Green Links Way, Green Jacket Crescent and Course View Street — 43 lots of 0.8 to 1.18 hectares (roughly two acres each), registration directed in February 2020. Phase 3 is all custom homes: lot buyers bring their own builders, so no two houses match, and the streetscape reads as a gallery of one-off estate builds rather than a builder’s row. It is the newest chapter of the Patterson story and the community’s luxury tier — covered in full in the Emerald Links Phase 3 standalone guide.

Living in Emerald Links

Living here means the course is your backyard and your social centre at once: members and neighbours meet at the clubhouse, the range and the academy, and the streets run quiet between tee times. Servicing is the Greely standard — private wells and septic systems on estate lots — and the roads are the township grid: Mitch Owens Road along the north, Stagecoach Road to the east, Manotick Station Road to the west. The village core, with The Shoppes of Greely plaza, the community centre and the library branch, is about 4.3 km east on Parkway Road and Bank Street; Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31) runs north to the city, and downtown Ottawa is roughly 35 minutes by car in good traffic. The honest transit picture matches the rest of Greely: there is no regular OC Transpo route through the village — the Thursday Shopper Route 304 is the one exception — so Emerald Links is a driver’s address, and school buses are the transit that matters to families here.

Emerald Links questions, answered

What is Emerald Links?
A 27-hole golf-course community in Greely, in Ottawa’s rural southeast — three registered phases of estate homes built around the Emerald Links Golf & Country Club by the Patterson family, who opened the course in July 1990. Phase 1 dates to 1988, Phase 2 (Pebblewoods Drive) to 2005, and Phase 3 — the custom-home phase — to 2020.

What is Emerald Links Phase 3?
The community’s newest phase (Plan 4M-1660), south of Pebblewoods Drive: 43 estate lots of 0.8 to 1.18 hectares — roughly two acres each — on Green Links Way, Green Jacket Crescent and Course View Street. Every Phase 3 home is a custom build; lot owners bring their own builders. It has its own guide on this site, linked from the listings section above.

Who builds the homes in Emerald Links?
In the established phases, the homes are resales of estate builds from the community’s building years. In Phase 3, every home is custom — Patterson Homes Inc., the Patterson family’s building arm based right in the community, builds here, and lot owners also bring their own custom builders.

Is Emerald Links on city water and sewers?
No — Emerald Links homes run on private wells and septic systems, the Greely standard for estate lots. The village’s one city-serviced pocket is Shadow Ridge and Lakeland Meadows, on the City’s communal systems, about 3.6 km east.

Which schools serve Emerald Links?
The designated paths are set out in the schools section above: Greely Elementary School and Castor Valley Elementary School on the public side, Osgoode Township High School for Grades 9 to 12, and St. Mary School to St. Mark Catholic High School on the Catholic side. Confirm any exact address in the board’s school locator before you buy.

How far is Emerald Links from downtown Ottawa?
About 30 kilometres — roughly 35 minutes by car in good traffic, up Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31). There is no regular OC Transpo route through the village; the Thursday Shopper Route 304 is the one exception.
