The Catchment, Street by Street

The school itself stands on Bilberry Drive in Orléans Woods, in the catchment’s northwest. Around it, the boundary takes in Convent Glen and Orléans Woods whole — Jeanne d’Arc Boulevard on both sides down to Orléans Boulevard, the St. Joseph Boulevard strip, and Place d’Orléans Drive at the top. North of the school, Chapel Hill and Chapel Hill South are inside, up to the river: the Forest Valley streets, Pagé Road and the Bilberry Creek ravine lands.

The southwest corner is the headline of the current map. Blackburn Hamlet — the whole hamlet inside the Green’s Creek loop, Innes Road on both sides, Bearbrook Road, Orient Park Drive, Tauvette Street and the Westpark streets — moved into St. Matthew in September 2025. For decades the hamlet pathed to Lester B. Pearson; on the current board map it is St. Matthew’s, and the Good Shepherd elementary area is what moved.

East of the school, the line takes in Bilberry Creek, Châteauneuf and Hiawatha Park, and runs out to the old Gloucester–Cumberland municipal line along Mer Bleue Road — where a handful of streets, Bourcier Drive, Prestone Drive and Legrand Crescent among them, split house by house between St. Matthew and St. Peter. Along the river, the west end of Cumberland village is inside, and Tweddle Road is inside in whole. South of Innes Road the catchment turns rural: Bradley Estates and the farm country down to the Concession 5/6 lot line, with Navan Road carried on both sides as far as Bradley Estates and the north end of Farmers Way inside.

The edges matter as much as the middle. Fallingbrook and Queenswood Heights, east of the line, are St. Peter’s. Mer Bleue Road south of Navan Road is St. Peter’s. West of Blair Road — and Innes Road west of Blair — the English Catholic secondary is Lester B. Pearson. The Russell Road side paths to St. Francis Xavier, and Farmers Way south of the lot line paths to St. Mark in Manotick. On any street near those edges, the exact address decides — which is what the free guide below is for.

St. Matthew Catholic High School — The Facts

St. Matthew Catholic High School, at 6550 Bilberry Drive — (613) 837-3161 — is the Ottawa Catholic School Board’s English Catholic secondary school for the western half of Orléans and, since September 2025, for Blackburn Hamlet. It teaches Grades 7 to 12 in one building, so a child who arrives in Grade 7 stays through graduation. The school opened in 1981 as the Eastern Area Junior High School, and it carried Orléans’ English Catholic students alone until St. Peter Catholic High School opened in 1992 and took over the eastern half of the suburb.

The boundary this guide is built on is the current one. In January 2025 the board’s trustees approved moving the Good Shepherd elementary school area — Blackburn Hamlet — from Lester B. Pearson Catholic High School to St. Matthew, effective September 2025, to balance enrolment between the two schools. If you are working from an older map, an older listing description or an older version of this guide series, Blackburn Hamlet is the change that matters most: the hamlet’s students now path to St. Matthew.

The School Pathway Into St. Matthew

The path, in one line: OCSB elementary school, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6 → St. Matthew Catholic High School, Grades 7 to 12.

Five Ottawa Catholic School Board elementary schools feed St. Matthew, named on the board’s own boundary map for the school. Each teaches Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6:

Chapel Hill Catholic School — 1534 Forest Valley Drive. The feeder for Chapel Hill and the Forest Valley streets in the catchment’s north.

Convent Glen Catholic School — 6212 Jeanne d’Arc Boulevard. The feeder for Convent Glen and Orléans Woods, the neighbourhoods wrapped around the high school itself.

Divine Infant School — 8100 Jeanne d’Arc Boulevard. The feeder for Bilberry Creek and Châteauneuf in the east of the catchment.

Good Shepherd Catholic School — 101 Bearbrook Road, Blackburn Hamlet. The feeder for the whole hamlet — and the school whose area moved from Lester B. Pearson to St. Matthew in the September 2025 boundary revision.

St. Kateri Tekakwitha School — 6400 Beausejour Drive. The feeder for the Beausejour Drive streets at the catchment’s southeast edge.

Which feeder serves your child is decided by your exact address, not by which school is closest — and on split streets near the boundary, two houses doors apart can feed different schools. Confirm the address before you buy, every time.

The Fraser Rating — One Measure, In Context

The Fraser Institute’s 2025 Report Card on Ontario’s secondary schools rates St. Matthew Catholic High School 6.9 out of 10, ranked #238 of 747 Ontario secondary schools, on 2023/24 data. That makes St. Matthew the higher-rated of Orléans’ two English Catholic secondaries — St. Peter sits at 6.5 — and it places the school mid-pack among Orléans secondaries overall: the French-language schools rate higher (Garneau 7.9, Mer Bleue 7.6, Béatrice-Desloges 7.5), and the English public schools Sir Wilfrid Laurier (6.1) and Cairine Wilson (5.9) rate lower.

Treat the rating as one measure, because that is what it is: it is built largely on provincial test results, and it says nothing about the teachers your child will actually get, the teams and clubs, or the feel of the building on Bilberry Drive. Read it, then walk the school and talk to families whose children go there — that is the research that settles it.

St. Matthew or St. Peter? The Orléans English Catholic Choice

Orléans has two English Catholic secondary schools, and the choice between them is made by your address, not by application. St. Matthew takes the west and north of Orléans — Convent Glen, Orléans Woods, Chapel Hill, Châteauneuf — plus Blackburn Hamlet and the Cumberland shore. St. Peter takes the east: Fallingbrook, Queenswood Heights and the Mer Bleue Road corridor south of Navan Road. Both are Ottawa Catholic School Board schools teaching Grades 7 to 12; on the Fraser Institute’s 2025 Report Card, St. Matthew rates 6.9 and St. Peter 6.5.

The practical point for buyers: the line between the two runs through ordinary suburban streets in east Orléans, and on the split streets — Bourcier Drive, Prestone Drive, Legrand Crescent — it is drawn house by house.
