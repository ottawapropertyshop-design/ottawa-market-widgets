One community, eleven neighbourhoods

Barrhaven grew from farmland south of the Greenbelt into one of Ottawa's largest suburban communities, and it still organises itself around a few anchors: Greenbank Road and Strandherd Drive carry the traffic, the Marketplace plaza at Strandherd and Greenbank covers the big weekly shop, and Walter Baker Sports Centre anchors the community's recreation. The Vimy Memorial Bridge ties the southern neighbourhoods across the Rideau River to Riverside South, and the oldest streets — Pheasant Run, Knollsbrook, Heritage Park — sit beside growth areas like Half Moon Bay that are still being built out.

Parks, play and daily life

Walter Baker Sports Centre is the community's living room — pools, rinks, the library branch and registered programs under one roof — and the neighbourhood parks, the Greenbelt edge and the Rideau River shoreline do the outdoor work. Daily life runs on the corridors: Strandherd Drive and Greenbank Road for shopping and transit, Woodroffe Avenue north to the rest of the city, and the Strandherd transit stations tying the community into the bus network.

Something interesting about Barrhaven

The community takes its name from Barrhaven Farm — and within living memory this was all farmland south of the Greenbelt. The eleven neighbourhoods in this week's count were fields, farm lots and a railway siding; the shelf of 189 listings stands where crops grew, which is why Barrhaven's oldest streets are barely a generation old and its newest are still being framed.
