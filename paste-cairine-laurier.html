The Short Version

Orléans has two public secondary schools in the Ottawa-Carleton District School Board, and their catchments do not sit side by side — one wraps around the other. Cairine Wilson Secondary School, at 975 Orléans Boulevard, is the designated school for old Orléans — Orléans Wood, Convent Glen, Queenswood Village — and for the whole rural east out to Boundary Road: a 315-square-kilometre catchment holding 23,978 civic addresses on 705 streets. Inside that wrap sits Sir Wilfrid Laurier Secondary School, at 1515 Tenth Line Road, designated for the newer subdivisions — Fallingbrook and most of Avalon — with 23,493 civic addresses on 538 streets.

Two honest warnings before the listings. First, the two lines overlap on their shared edge: about 300 addresses, on streets like Trail Side Circle, fall inside both digitized board maps, and only the board’s locator can settle those. Second, the pair does not cover everything between them: about 660 addresses in a south-Avalon strip below the Innes Road and Brian Coburn Boulevard line fall in neither catchment in this guide. This page never promises an address to a school the board has not assigned it — it shows you both lines, both markets, and the differences that actually matter, and it tells you exactly how to confirm your address.

Cairine Wilson Secondary School

Cairine Wilson Secondary School opened in 1975 and is named for Cairine Wilson, Canada’s first female senator. It is the main English-language public secondary school for eastern Orléans: about 820 students in Grades 9 to 12, home of the Wildcats, running the English program and French Immersion (with DELF certification), Advanced Placement classes in Grades 11 and 12, the International Certificate Program, and an Outdoor Education program with canoe trips and wilderness certifications, alongside co-operative education, OYAP and dual-credit options. Its Specialist High Skills Major is in the Environment sector — “Global and Indigenous Environment Lenses” in the board’s own program table.

Cairine Wilson Secondary School at 975 Orléans Boulevard.

The catchment is two markets wearing one name. West of the enclave it is the established suburb: Orléans Wood’s and Convent Glen’s streets north of Highway 174, Queenswood Village and the old village along St-Joseph Boulevard, the Fallingbrook fringe outside the Laurier line and Avalon’s west edge. East and south it is another world entirely — Cumberland Village, Sarsfield and the Cumberland concessions, acreages and rural roads running south to Mitch Owens Road and east to Boundary Road. A family can hold a Cairine Wilson address on a quiet cul-de-sac ten minutes from Place d’Orléans, or on a concession road with the school bus as the morning plan. Both are in this guide, because both are in the board’s boundary.

Sir Wilfrid Laurier Secondary School

Sir Wilfrid Laurier Secondary School stands at 1515 Tenth Line Road, beside the Ray Friel Recreation Complex, in the middle of the neighbourhood it serves. It is a semestered Grades 9 to 12 school — the Lancers, motto “We Strive for the Highest” — with about 1,050 students in 2024-25 by the Ontario Ministry of Education’s published figures. It runs the English program and an extensive French Immersion program, a strong Arts offering across music, drama and visual arts, technology and leadership courses, co-operative education and OYAP. Its two Specialist High Skills Majors are Information and Communications Technology — Broadcasting or Digital Media — and Arts and Culture, and its Grade 12 broadcasting students can earn a Dual Credit with Algonquin College. What it does not offer is Advanced Placement or the International Certificate Program; in this pair of schools, those are Cairine Wilson’s.

Princess Louise Falls, in Fallingbrook — the Laurier catchment’s own backyard waterfall. Photo: Tomasz R. Witek

The catchment is the new Orléans: Fallingbrook first — the bulk of it, around the school itself — and most of Avalon to the south, with Queenswood Heights’ southern streets at the north edge. These are the subdivisions built from the 1990s onward: crescents and courts of detached homes, semis and townhouses, close to Tenth Line Road’s schools, arenas and shopping. Nearly every address in this catchment is a short drive — or a walk — from the school, which is the opposite of its wrap-around neighbour, and the first thing to understand when you compare the two.

Cairine Wilson or Sir Wilfrid Laurier? What Actually Differs

Start with geography, because geography is destiny here. Cairine Wilson takes the older Orléans and the rural east: a family in its catchment might live in a 1970s Orléans Wood bungalow, an old-village home on St-Joseph Boulevard, or a farmhouse on a Cumberland concession — and their teenager’s commute could be a walk, a city bus, or a long rural school-bus ride. Laurier takes the newer subdivisions: Fallingbrook and most of Avalon, where the school sits in the middle of its own catchment and most students live within a short drive of Tenth Line Road. Same suburb, two very different mornings.

Programs are the second difference, and the one that should drive a school-first move. Cairine Wilson is the academic-extensions school of the pair: Advanced Placement in Grades 11 and 12, the International Certificate Program, DELF certification inside its French Immersion stream, Outdoor Education, and an Environment SHSM. Laurier is the media-and-arts school: an ICT SHSM in Broadcasting or Digital Media, an Arts and Culture SHSM, a strong arts program across music, drama and visual arts, and a Grade 12 Dual Credit in broadcasting with Algonquin College. Neither hosts a congregated gifted class. A student set on broadcasting has a clear answer in this pair; so does a student set on AP courses — and they are opposite answers, which is exactly why this comparison exists.

On ratings, be suspicious of anyone who crowns a winner. The Fraser Institute’s 2025 Report Card (2023/24 data) puts Cairine Wilson at 5.9 out of 10 with a five-year rating of 6.8, ranked 401 of 747 Ontario secondary schools, and Sir Wilfrid Laurier at 6.1 with a five-year rating of 6.7, ranked 367 of 747. That is a wash by any honest reading — two-tenths of a point in a single year, five-year bands that are effectively identical. I publish both numbers because buyers ask for them, with their bands attached. The fuller picture is the program list, a school visit, and the board’s own reporting.

Where the kids come from: the board publishes no single official feeder list for either school, so what follows is only what board and trustee sources state. Into Cairine Wilson: Henry Larsen Elementary School names Cairine Wilson among its graduates’ destinations (the destination varies by address and program), and Orléans Wood Elementary School’s own page describes its students continuing to Terry Fox Elementary School and then on to Cairine Wilson. Into Sir Wilfrid Laurier: Fallingbrook Community Elementary School, Dunning-Foubert Elementary School, Trillium Elementary School and Maple Ridge Elementary School are each stated as feeding Laurier on the Ward 1 trustee’s school pages, and Dunning-Foubert’s path is confirmed in the board’s own East Urban consultation notes. One live wrinkle: that same East Urban study is currently reviewing Grade 7–8 feeding patterns in exactly this area, so treat feeder paths as today’s pattern, never as a promise — the catchment itself is the designation that matters.

And the edges, one more time, because they are where money is lost. About 300 addresses sit inside both schools’ digitized lines — Trail Side Circle is the famous case, where the board’s line weaves down the street itself — and about 660 addresses in south Avalon, below the Innes Road and Brian Coburn Boulevard line, sit in neither catchment in this guide. Boundary streets divide by side and segment: Tenth Line Road, Mer-Bleue Road, Old Montreal Road and Wall Road among them. On any of those streets, the only honest sentence is “the locator decides” — and the time to run it is before the offer, not after.

Can a Student Cross the Line?

Living on one side of the line and wanting the other school is possible, but it is an application, not a choice. Under the OCDSB’s designated-schools and transfer policy, every student’s designated school is set by home address and program — and because both of these schools publish one combined English-and-Immersion boundary, French Immersion creates no crossing right between them. To cross, you first register your child at the designated school, then apply to the receiving school on the board’s Form 118S during its annual two-week window — January 26 to February 6, 2026 for the 2026-27 school year — showing a reason the policy recognizes: the student’s particular learning needs, a course package (a sequential three-course series) not offered at the designated school, or exceptional personal circumstances. Approval is discretionary, decisions arrive by the end of March, and transportation is not provided for an approved transfer — the drive is on the family.

The exception is programs. A seat in a Specialist High Skills Major is a system-program placement, not a boundary transfer: a Cairine Wilson-area student accepted into Laurier’s Broadcasting or Arts and Culture SHSM, or a Laurier-area student accepted into Cairine Wilson’s Environment SHSM, attends under that program route (Laurier’s own page describes its SHSM delivery as one semester at the host school, then back to the home school for the remaining diploma requirements). Cairine Wilson’s AP and International Certificate, which Laurier does not offer, feed the “course package” transfer ground rather than any automatic right. And there are no option (choice) areas between these two schools — the board publishes no option sheet for either. It is designated school, transfer application, or program placement: those are the three doors, and this guide’s job is to make sure you know which one your address holds before you buy.

Questions Families Ask

Two public high schools serve Orléans — which one is ours?
The one your exact address is designated to, and in Orléans the answer surprises people: Cairine Wilson Secondary School’s boundary wraps all the way around Sir Wilfrid Laurier Secondary School’s. Old Orléans, Orléans Wood, Convent Glen and the entire rural east belong to Cairine Wilson; the Fallingbrook and Avalon core inside the wrap belongs to Laurier. This guide maps both board lines exactly as published, and the check that settles any address is the board’s school locator — never the neighbourhood name alone.

We are looking in Fallingbrook or Avalon — which school should we expect?
Most of Fallingbrook and most of Avalon are Sir Wilfrid Laurier’s catchment — the school stands on Tenth Line Road, inside the neighbourhood. But the edges matter: Avalon’s west edge and the Fallingbrook fringe outside the enclave line are Cairine Wilson’s, and a strip of south Avalon below the Innes Road and Brian Coburn Boulevard line is in neither catchment in this guide — those addresses are designated elsewhere. Price the home on the home, and confirm the school for the exact address before you write an offer.

Can an address be in both catchments at once?
On the shared edge, yes — about 300 addresses on streets like Trail Side Circle sit inside both digitized board lines, because the two sheets genuinely draw the line down the same streets. Neither guide nor any seller can promise those addresses to one school or the other; the board’s locator decides address by address. If you are buying on a shared-edge street, get the designation in writing before the offer is firm.

What is actually different between the two schools?
Programs and geography, mostly. Cairine Wilson offers Advanced Placement in Grades 11 and 12, the International Certificate Program, Outdoor Education and an Environment SHSM. Sir Wilfrid Laurier offers two SHSMs — Information and Communications Technology (Broadcasting / Digital Media) and Arts and Culture — a strong Arts program and a Dual Credit broadcasting course with Algonquin College, but no AP and no International Certificate. Cairine Wilson draws from old Orléans plus a 315-square-kilometre rural east; Laurier draws from the newer subdivisions. The full comparison is in its own section above.

Which school rates higher?
Honestly, it is a wash. The Fraser Institute’s 2025 Report Card (2023/24 data) rates Cairine Wilson 5.9 out of 10 (five-year rating 6.8, ranked 401 of 747) and Sir Wilfrid Laurier 6.1 out of 10 (five-year rating 6.7, ranked 367 of 747). A two-tenths gap in one year on test-based measures is not a verdict on either school — read the programs, visit both, and treat the rating as one input among several.

Can my child attend the other school instead?
Possibly, but it is an application, not a choice. Under the OCDSB’s transfer policy you first register at your designated school, then apply to the receiving school on Form 118S during the board’s annual window — January 26 to February 6, 2026 for the 2026-27 school year — showing a learning-needs, course-package or exceptional-circumstances reason. Decisions are discretionary and arrive by the end of March, and transportation is not provided for an approved transfer. The exception is programs: an SHSM seat — Broadcasting at Laurier, Environment at Cairine Wilson — is a system-program placement, not a boundary transfer.

Does French Immersion change which school we get?
No. Both schools publish one combined English-and-Immersion boundary, so an address’s French Immersion school is the same building as its English school. Wanting French Immersion never moves a Cairine Wilson address to Laurier, or the reverse.
