The community — Cedar Lake Estates on Cedarlakes Way

Cedarlakes — Cedar Lake Estates on the registered plans — was built by Sunset Lakes Developments, through its Cedar Lakes company (6980848 Canada Corporation), in two phases registered as Plans 4M-1479 and 4M-1555. It sits off Stagecoach Road in Greely’s southwest, and it is deliberately small: Cedarlakes Way carries the community — every address on the street, from the Stagecoach Road junction to the lake end — and the pocket closes at its northwest corner with the four houses at the south end of Spartan Grove, below Courtland Grove Crescent. Only that southern part of Spartan Grove is Cedarlakes; the rest of the street, north of Courtland Grove Crescent, is not. Courtland Grove Crescent is not a Cedarlakes street either: its lots sit outside Plans 4M-1479 and 4M-1555, which between them hold Cedarlakes Way lots only — Lots 1 to 40 on 4M-1479 and Lots 1 to 20 on 4M-1555 — and that includes the first two lots on the crescent’s south side, checked against the plans for this guide.

The selling feature here is what is behind the house: Cedar Lake itself. The homes on the lake side of Cedarlakes Way back onto the water — lots running down to the shore, no rear neighbours, and the Cedarlakes Association running the dock and lake access for residents. The streetscape is executive resale — custom homes on generous lots — rather than a community still under construction, so what comes to market is a known quantity: mature trees, finished landscaping, and a lake that has been the centre of the neighbourhood since the first phase registered.

Living in Cedarlakes

Daily life here starts with the water: Cedar Lake at the back of the lot, the association dock and lake access, and one quiet street with no through traffic. The village core is about 4.0 km north — The Shoppes of Greely plaza on Bank Street for groceries and everyday services, the Greely Community Centre and the library branch on Meadow Drive. Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31) is the spine north: downtown Ottawa is roughly half an hour in good traffic. The honest transit picture matches the rest of Greely: no regular OC Transpo route runs through the village — the Thursday Shopper Route 304 is the one exception — so Cedarlakes is a driver’s address, and school buses, run by the Ottawa Student Transportation Authority, are the transit that matters to families here.
