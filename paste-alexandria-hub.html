Alexandria (719) — the Priest’s-Mill Town

Alexandria exists because of a mill. In 1804 the Reverend Alexander Macdonell bought land on the banks of the Garry River — named for Loch Garry in Scotland — and in 1819 built a modest wooden grist mill there; the settlement’s first Gaelic name was Muileann an t-Sagairt, “Priest’s Mill.” The mill, later rebuilt in stone on the present Priest’s Mill site in Mill Square, anchored the town that took Macdonell’s own first name — Alexandria — and was incorporated in 1902. The stone mill building still stands, now the Priest’s Mill Arts Centre, and the town still organizes itself around it: Main Street with the St. Finnan’s spire in the banner photograph at the top of this page, the lake and the mill pond, the arena, and the schools.

For buyers, Alexandria is the hub’s service town: the place with the high schools — Glengarry District High School and Le Relais stand about five hundred metres apart — the hospital services, the shops and the VIA-era main street, wrapped in streets of century homes, postwar family houses and newer builds at the edges. It is also the hub’s only real condominium address and its natural first stop: most Glengarry buyers start their search here and work outward to the villages and concessions as the budget and the wish list negotiate.

Maxville (718) — the Highland Games Village

Maxville’s main street — a farm-service village that fills a fairground every August. Photo: P199, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

Maxville, 17.2 kilometres west of Alexandria in Kenyon Township, is a compact farm-service village with an outsized summer. The Glengarry Highland Games, first staged in 1948 by local businessmen determined to keep Scottish traditions alive for the younger generation, are now among the largest Highland Games outside Scotland — twenty thousand and more through the Maxville Fairgrounds on the first weekend of August, home to the North American Pipe Band Championships, the massed bands, the heavy events and the caber. The rest of the year, Maxville is the quiet version: village streets, the fairgrounds, the arena, and Maxville Public School — whose split high-school path (most of its boundary to Tagwi, the eastern strip to Glengarry District High School) is explained plainly in the Schools section, because it changes what a Maxville address actually buys a family.

Kenyon Township (720) — Dunvegan and the Western Concessions

Kenyon Township wraps Maxville and runs west and north from Alexandria — Dunvegan, 14.8 kilometres northwest of the town, is its other named place, a crossroads hamlet with deep Highland roots (its name is a Skye castle’s, carried here by settlers). The township is the hub’s western farm country: dairy and cash-crop concessions, farmhouses and country properties on quiet roads, with Maxville’s services at one end and Alexandria’s at the other. School-wise the township rides the northern paths — Laggan to Glengarry District High School for most addresses, with the Maxville Public School split touching its eastern side — address by address, as always out here.

Lochiel Township (721) — Laggan, Kirk Hill and the North Country

Lochiel Township is the hub’s north country — the concessions above Alexandria holding Laggan (9.4 kilometres), Kirk Hill and Dalkeith. It is named for the Cameron of Lochiel country its settlers came from, and it still reads that way: rolling farm country, big skies, and Laggan Public School standing on County Road 24 as the township’s anchor institution. Lochiel is the purest version of the northern school path — Laggan Public School to Glengarry District High School — and the purest version of township buying: space, quiet and concession-road properties, with Alexandria’s main street ten minutes south.

Lancaster Village (722) — the Lake St. Francis Shore

Lancaster village — the county’s front door on Lake St. Francis. Photo: P199, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

Lancaster, 22.5 kilometres southeast of Alexandria, is Glengarry’s waterfront village — the first Ontario community west of the Quebec border on the old King’s Highway, sitting where the county meets Lake St. Francis. The village trades on the water: marinas, the shore road, summer places turning into year-round homes, and a main street that serves both the village and the township concessions behind it. Its children ride the southern school path — Williamstown Public School to Char-Lan District High School — and its buyers cross-shop the shore from Green Valley to Bainsville as one waterfront market.

Charlottenburgh Township (723) — Williamstown, Green Valley and St. Raphael’s

Charlottenburgh is the hub’s history township. Williamstown, 19.6 kilometres south of Alexandria, is one of Ontario’s oldest Loyalist settlements: Sir John Johnson, the Loyalist leader, built his manor house here in the 1780s — among Ontario’s oldest surviving buildings and a National Historic Site — and in 1814 granted the fairground acres where the Williamstown Fair, patented in 1808 and on its present grounds since 1812, still runs as Canada’s oldest annual fair. Williamstown also holds the hub’s neatest school arrangement: Williamstown Public School and Char-Lan District High School stand across the street from each other on County Road 17 — a genuine kindergarten-to-graduation campus. This township carries the hub’s promoted spoke: the Williamstown Ontario Homes for Sale Neighbourhood Guide goes deep on the village, the fair and the Char-Lan path.

East of Williamstown, Green Valley (7.4 kilometres south of Alexandria) and the township’s river-side concessions run toward the Lancaster shore. Green Valley and Bainsville-area buyers carry one extra piece of homework, covered in the Schools section: their old elementary school, S.J. McLeod, is closed, and their addresses now split between the Laggan–Glengarry path and the Williamstown–Char-Lan path. And west of the village stands St. Raphael’s: the great stone church built 1815–1821 for the Highland Catholic parish Fr. Alexander Macdonell led — for decades the largest Catholic parish in Upper Canada, run by the colony’s first Catholic bishop. Fire took the roof and interior in 1970; the walls stand as the St. Raphael’s Ruins, a National Historic Site and the single most evocative place in the county.

Lancaster Township (724) — North Lancaster, Bainsville and the Border Country

Lancaster Township is the hub’s eastern and northern shore country: North Lancaster (13.6 kilometres from Alexandria) inland, and Bainsville (23.3 kilometres) on the Lake St. Francis shore at the Quebec border. The township mixes working farms, shore properties and border-country hamlets, and it rides the southern school path — Williamstown Public School to Char-Lan District High School — with the eastern end of the old S.J. McLeod area folded into that same path after the school’s closure. For buyers, this is the hub’s value-and-water corner: Lake St. Francis frontage and big rural lots, with Lancaster village’s services at hand and Alexandria’s up County Road 34.

A Short History — Three Founding Stories

Glengarry was settled twice in the same decade, by two peoples who between them gave the county its character. In 1784 the Loyalists came: disbanded soldiers and refugee families granted land along the St. Lawrence after the American Revolution, with Sir John Johnson’s manor at Williamstown as their seat. Two years later, in 1786, came the Highland Scots — some five hundred of them, mostly Macdonells, led by their priest, Fr. Alexander Macdonell, settling the interior townships and building St. Raphael’s into the cradle of Catholicism in Ontario. Macdonell himself founded Alexandria on his mill in 1819 and became the first Roman Catholic bishop of Upper Canada. French-Canadian families followed through the nineteenth century, and the county’s blend — Gaelic, Loyalist English and French — is still its living texture.

The townships buyers deal with today are the modern frame over that fabric: North Glengarry (Alexandria, Maxville, Kenyon and Lochiel) and South Glengarry (Charlottenburgh and Lancaster townships and Lancaster village), formed in the 1998 amalgamations. The districts in this guide are the survey townships underneath — which is why one hub can hold a mill town, a fair village, a games village and four farm townships, and still read as one market with one story.

Servicing — the Glengarry Homework

Alexandria, Maxville, Williamstown and Lancaster are serviced or partially serviced communities wrapped in rural townships, and the line between the two is a lot line, not a guess. Before any purchase in the hub:

  • Confirm the services for the exact lot with the municipality — North Glengarry or South Glengarry, depending on the address — whether the property connects to municipal water and sewer or runs on a private system, and what connection charges apply. Never assume from the street name or a boundary on a map.

  • On a well and septic property — the norm across the townships — budget for a potability and bacteria water test, a flow check, and a septic inspection with the tank pumped and the bed assessed. A replacement bed is one of the largest cheques a rural buyer can inherit.

  • Heating history matters — oil, propane, electric and wood heat are all common out here, and each carries a very different monthly reality through an Eastern Ontario winter.

  • For century homes, look hard at foundation, wiring and insulation before conditions come off. Charm and knob-and-tube are frequent roommates in nineteenth-century stock.

  • On shore lots along Lake St. Francis, ask about shoreline road allowances, flood history and erosion specifically — standard questions on any waterfront purchase in the townships.

None of this is a reason to avoid the hub — two municipalities’ worth of families live happily on some version of it. It is a reason to buy with your eyes open, and it is standard practice in every offer I write out here.

Schools — the Paths That Organize This Hub

This is the fact that organizes the whole hub, and the reason this ring of guides is built the way it is: the seven districts group themselves by where their children go to school. The paths, stated one clear line at a time:

  • The northern public path: Laggan Public School → Glengarry District High School. Laggan Public School (Upper Canada District School Board), 20345 County Road 24, Dalkeith — Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6, offering Core French and French Immersion, the rural school for Lochiel Township. Its students continue to Glengarry District High School (UCDSB), 212 Main Street North, Alexandria — Grades 7 to 12, a school established in 1865 with French Immersion and specialist programs. This path serves Alexandria, Lochiel Township and Kenyon Township — and, since 2017, the northern strip of the former S.J. McLeod boundary, named below.

  • The southern public path: Williamstown Public School → Char-Lan District High School. Williamstown Public School (UCDSB), 19754 County Road 17, Williamstown — Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6. Directly across the street stands Char-Lan District High School (UCDSB), 19743 John Street, Williamstown — Grades 7 to 12, the current building opened in 1954, with French Immersion in all grades. This path serves Charlottenburgh Township, Lancaster Township and Lancaster village.

  • S.J. McLeod Public School is closed — read this if you are buying around Green Valley or Bainsville. S.J. McLeod was the elementary school for the Green Valley side of South Glengarry, and older listings still name it. It is not operating: following the Upper Canada board’s 2016–17 accommodation review, the school closed, and effective September 2017 the northern portion of its attendance boundary was consolidated with Laggan Public School — putting those homes on the Laggan → Glengarry District High School path — while the rest of its former area feeds Williamstown Public School and then Char-Lan District High School. Two live paths now cover its old ground; which one serves an exact address is the board locator’s call.

  • The Maxville split, stated plainly. Maxville Public School (UCDSB), 15 Alexander Street West, Maxville — Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6. Most of its boundary feeds Tagwi Secondary School, the high school in the Cornwall hub’s ring. The eastern strip of the Maxville boundary is the exception: those students attend Glengarry District High School in Alexandria for Grades 7 to 12. Same elementary school, two high schools — confirm the exact address before you buy.

  • The French-Catholic path, complete inside Alexandria: École élémentaire catholique Elda-Rouleau → École secondaire catholique Le Relais. Elda-Rouleau (Conseil scolaire de district catholique de l’Est ontarien), 115 Sandfield Avenue South, Alexandria — Kindergarten to Grade 6. Its students continue to Le Relais (CSDCEO), 100 McNab Street, Alexandria — Grades 7 to 12. A full French-Catholic education without leaving town — one of Alexandria’s quiet strengths for francophone families.

  • English-Catholic elementary schools: St. Finnan’s Catholic School (Catholic District School Board of Eastern Ontario), 220 Main Street, Alexandria — Junior Kindergarten to Grade 8, with French Immersion; and Iona Academy Catholic School (CDSBEO), 20019 King’s Road, Williamstown — Junior Kindergarten to Grade 8, Immersion. The boards do not publish a single designated secondary feeder for these two schools in the sources this guide relies on, so no feeder is claimed here — Catholic families should confirm the secondary assignment for their exact address with the CDSBEO directly.

Read the paths the way a family lives them: in the north, a village elementary school in Laggan and the bus into Alexandria’s high school; in the south, two schools facing each other across County Road 17 in Williamstown from kindergarten to graduation; in Maxville, one elementary school and a boundary line that decides between two towns’ high schools; and in Alexandria itself, public, Catholic and French-Catholic paths all inside the town limits. That is why the guides in this program are organized by school catchments first — where your little one goes to primary school and where their older sister goes to secondary is the question every buying family actually asks.

This information is provided for general guidance only. School catchment boundaries can change, and school assignment is always the school board’s decision. Before purchasing a home, buyers should confirm the schools assigned to the exact address — with the board’s school locator and directly with any other school they are considering.

Churches & Community Life

The hub’s calendar runs on the institutions its communities built and kept: the Glengarry Highland Games and the Maxville Fair in Maxville; the Williamstown Fair on its 1812 grounds; hockey and community sport at the arenas in Alexandria, Maxville and Lancaster; the Priest’s Mill Arts Centre in Mill Square; and the parishes — St. Finnan’s in Alexandria, the modern church beside the St. Raphael’s ruins, and the Anglican, United and Presbyterian congregations that hold their corners of every village. A county founded by a priest, a Loyalist colonel and five hundred Highlanders does not coast on community spirit — it manufactures it, on purpose, every season.

Getting to Ottawa — and Around the County

Alexandria sits on County Road 34, the county’s north-south spine, with County Road 43 running west through Maxville toward Winchester and Kemptville, County Road 17 tying Williamstown and Lancaster along the south, and Highway 401 across the county’s southern edge with the 417 beyond Cornwall’s side of the map. Lancaster is 22.5 straight-line kilometres southeast, Williamstown 19.6 south, Maxville 17.2 west; Cornwall is about half an hour south, Montreal about an hour east, and downtown Ottawa about an hour and a half by road. The honest transit picture: there is no commuter transit to Ottawa from Glengarry. Between communities this is a car county, full stop — plan on driving for work, shopping and appointments, with school buses for the kids. For most buyers here that is not a compromise; it is the trade they came for.

Questions Buyers Ask About Alexandria and Glengarry

Is Alexandria, Ontario a good place to live?
For the right buyer, Glengarry is the most distinctive county in Eastern Ontario. Alexandria gives you a complete small town — schools in three systems, shops, the arena, the lake and the mill pond — and around it a ring of villages and farm country where a detached home costs a fraction of the Ottawa equivalent. The honest trade-offs: daily life runs on a car, the drive to Ottawa is a real highway run, and the rental shelf is thin because this is an ownership county. Buyers who choose Glengarry are usually choosing space, heritage and community on purpose — the Highland Games, the Williamstown Fair and a main street that still works are the texture of the place, not a brochure line.

Which schools serve the Alexandria and Glengarry area?
The hub runs on clear paths. In the north, Laggan Public School (UCDSB, 20345 County Road 24, Dalkeith, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6) feeds Glengarry District High School in Alexandria (212 Main Street North, Grades 7 to 12) — that path serves Alexandria, Lochiel Township, Kenyon Township and the northern strip of the former S.J. McLeod boundary. In the south, Williamstown Public School (19754 County Road 17, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6) feeds Char-Lan District High School, right across the street in Williamstown (19743 John Street, Grades 7 to 12) — serving Charlottenburgh Township, Lancaster Township and Lancaster village. French-Catholic families in Alexandria have Elda-Rouleau (Kindergarten to Grade 6) feeding Le Relais (Grades 7 to 12), both in town; English-Catholic elementary schools are St. Finnan’s in Alexandria and Iona Academy in Williamstown. Always confirm the school assigned to an exact address with the board before you buy.

What happened to S.J. McLeod Public School?
It closed — and buyers around Green Valley and Bainsville need to know that, because old listings and older summaries still name it as the local school. Following the Upper Canada board’s 2016–17 accommodation review, S.J. McLeod closed, and effective September 2017 the northern portion of its attendance boundary was consolidated with Laggan Public School, which puts those homes on the Glengarry District High School path in Alexandria; the rest of its former area feeds Williamstown Public School and then Char-Lan District High School in Williamstown. There is no operating S.J. McLeod school today, so run any Green Valley or Bainsville address through the board locator and confirm which of the two live paths it sits on.

How does the Maxville Public School split work?
Maxville Public School (UCDSB, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6) is one elementary school with two secondary destinations. Most of its boundary feeds Tagwi Secondary School, the North Stormont high school covered in the Cornwall hub’s ring. The eastern strip of the Maxville boundary is the exception: students there attend Glengarry District High School in Alexandria for Grades 7 to 12. So two children on different concessions out of the same Maxville classroom can be headed to different high schools in different towns — which is exactly why the exact address, checked on the board locator, matters more than the village name on the listing.

How far is Alexandria from Ottawa?
About 84 kilometres straight-line from downtown — plan on roughly an hour and a half by road in normal conditions. The usual run is County Road 43 and the local highways west to Highway 417, then straight into the city; Cornwall and the 401 are about half an hour south, and Montreal is about an hour east. Winter adds its opinion, as it does everywhere in the counties, but there is no route-finding in the trip. Buyers here are not commuting daily in most cases — they are trading the daily drive for the house, the land and the county.

Are homes outside the towns on well and septic?
Almost always, yes — that is the honest answer for the township concessions, and it is one of the first checks on any purchase here. Alexandria, Maxville, Williamstown and Lancaster are serviced or partially serviced communities, but the line between municipal services and private systems is a lot line, not a guess. On a well and septic property, budget for a potability and bacteria water test, a flow check, and a septic inspection with the tank pumped and the bed assessed. Before you buy anywhere in the hub, confirm the services for the exact lot with the municipality — North Glengarry or South Glengarry, depending on the address.

What is Glengarry known for?
Three founding stories and two living traditions. Loyalist Williamstown, where Sir John Johnson built his manor house in the 1780s and the fair patented in 1808 still runs on its 1812 grounds — Canada’s oldest annual fair. Highland Catholic St. Raphael’s, whose great stone church of 1815–1821 is now the famous ruins, a National Historic Site. And Alexandria itself, the priest’s-mill town on the Garry River. Every summer the county fills for the Glengarry Highland Games in Maxville — among the largest Highland Games outside Scotland, home to the North American Pipe Band Championships — and, a week apart, the Williamstown Fair. Glengarry does not perform its Scottish heritage; it simply never stopped living it.

Why do some local homes for sale not appear on this website?
Because of how listings reach agent websites. This site is fed by the CREA Data Distribution Facility (DDF), and at upload the listing brokerage chooses whether a listing is distributed to sites like this one. Some brokerages in the counties opt out — those homes show on REALTOR.ca and in the MLS system but never reach this search, which is why the live panels here can run thinner than the real market. It is not a broken search; it is a distribution choice. If you are hunting in this hub, call me: I work from the full MLS picture, not just the distributed slice.
