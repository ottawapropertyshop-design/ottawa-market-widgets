Your First 90 Days at eXp Realty The Ramp Plan for Switching Agents

In short: Your first 90 days at eXp Realty as an already-licensed agent switching brokerages should not be spent learning everything at once. Weeks 1–2 are setup: licence transfer, systems, banking, branding, and client notification. Days 15–30 are first calls and reactivating your database. Days 31–60 are consistent lead generation. Days 61–90 are review, pipeline, and your first full month operating on the new cap year — which started on your anniversary date with an 80/20 split toward a $16,000 CAD cap at $80,000 GCI.

Who This Ramp Plan Is For

TL;DR: This is for a licensed agent with an existing business moving brokerages — not a brand-new licensee starting from zero.

If you already have clients, listings in progress, and a database, your risk in a switch is not ability. It is interruption. Deals must keep closing, clients must hear from you before they hear rumours, and your lead flow must not pause for a month while you learn new software.

This plan sequences the move so the business keeps running. It assumes the Canadian eXp structure set out on the hub at https://www.sellingottawa.realestate/blog/Fed+Up+With+Your+Brokerage+How+eXp+Realty+Actually+Works: 80/20 split, $16,000 CAD cap at $80,000 GCI, post-cap fees of $285 per deal for 20 deals (maximum $5,700) then $75 per deal, $139 monthly cloud fee, $199 startup fee, and $29 per deal for broker review. Your cap resets on your anniversary date, so your first 90 days are also the start of your first cap year — early production counts from day one.

Weeks 1–2: The Setup Checklist

TL;DR: Finish every administrative item in the first two weeks so nothing administrative is still open in week three.

Work through this list in order, and do not start rebranding projects until the first five items are done:

  • Licence and board transfer confirmed, with written dates for when you can trade under the new brokerage
  • Pending deals and listings documented: which brokerage each file closes under, and who to call with questions
  • Banking, direct deposit, tax, and payment details set up and tested
  • Email, CRM, transaction management, and e-signature logins working on your phone and computer
  • Your full client and sphere database exported from your old systems and imported — names, emails, phone numbers, notes
  • New brokerage branding applied to your email signature, social profiles, and listing materials — in that order
  • Every active client personally notified by you, by phone or in person where possible, before any public announcement
  • A simple one-page explanation ready for clients: what changed, what did not, and your unchanged phone number

Two habits matter in these two weeks. First, keep a single running list of open items and clear it daily; a switch generates dozens of small tasks and they are easy to lose. Second, book your training in small daily blocks rather than a single marathon day — you will retain the systems you use on real files that same week.

Days 15–30: First Calls and Database Reactivation

TL;DR: Your fastest early wins are already in your database. Call your sphere first, prospects second, cold leads last.

Once setup is complete, your first calls are not cold calls. Start with past clients, then your active buyers and sellers, then everyone who enquired in the last year and went quiet. The conversation is simple and honest: you have moved brokerages, your phone number and service are unchanged, the cloud-based model means you are reachable the same way, and you are checking in on their plans.

What should I say to clients about why I switched?

Keep it short, positive, and about service — never about your old brokerage. One or two sentences is enough: you moved to a cloud-based brokerage with over 82,000 agents across 29 countries, which gives your clients a wider referral network, and everything about how you work with them stays the same. Then move the conversation back to them and their plans.

By day 30 you should have spoken personally to every active client, touched your top 50 sphere contacts, and reactivated your database in your new CRM with every contact tagged by status. That tagging work feels slow; it is what makes the next 60 days possible.

Days 31–60: First Lead-Generation Steps

TL;DR: Pick two lead sources you will actually sustain, work them daily, and measure conversations — not clicks.

Agents who join eXp through Tomasz R. Witek and my senior partner as co-sponsors are set up for the Free Lead Generation program: the leads generated are 100% yours, and a weekly mastermind supports the system and the follow-up. Treat that program as one of your two sources, and pair it with the source you already know works for you — sphere, open houses, referrals, or content.

Your daily structure for days 31–60:

  • A fixed morning block for new conversations — calls, follow-ups, and database touches before email
  • Every new lead entered in the CRM the same day, with a next-step date
  • The weekly mastermind attended, with one idea implemented each week rather than notes collected
  • One piece of local content per week — a market update, a neighbourhood note, or a short video — reused across your profiles
  • A Friday 15-minute review: conversations held, appointments set, pipeline added

Revenue-share mechanics are not a lead-generation activity and should not take time in this window; if you want the full mechanics, they are set out in the explainer at https://www.sellingottawa.realestate/blog/eXp+Realty+Revenue+Share+Explained+The+7+Tier+System. Amounts vary and are not guaranteed — see eXp's income disclosure. Your first 90 days are for production.

How many systems do I need to learn in the first month?

Only the ones your next deal needs: transaction management, CRM, e-signature, and communication tools. Everything else can wait for days 31–90. Agents stall when they try to master every platform before making calls. Learn in the order your business demands, one system at a time, on live files.

Days 61–90: Review, Pipeline, and Your First Milestones

TL;DR: At 90 days you should be able to state your pipeline, your cap progress, and your weekly lead rhythm in one minute each.

Use these milestones as your scorecard:

  • 30 days: Transfer and setup fully closed, all active clients spoken to, database imported and tagged, first calls complete
  • 60 days: Two lead sources running weekly, mastermind rhythm established, first new-brokerage files opened, content publishing weekly
  • 90 days: A written pipeline with values and expected closing dates, your GCI tracked against the $80,000 cap threshold, fees reconciled against the known schedule — $139 monthly, $29 per deal — with no surprises

Also do a money check at day 90. Reconcile what you have actually been charged against what you expected from the fee schedule. A clean reconciliation builds confidence in the move; a surprise found at day 90 is a question you can still get answered quickly. In Q2 2026 eXp reported 87,338 agents including approximately 4,900 from NextHome — you are joining a large, established system, and using its support channels early is part of the plan, not a sign of trouble.

Finally, set your next 90 days before this period ends: one production goal, one database goal, and one skill goal. The agents who do well after a switch are rarely the ones with the most dramatic first month. They are the ones whose second quarter looks like a system, not a restart.
