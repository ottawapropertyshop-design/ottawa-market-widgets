Ottawa's historic mill village

Manotick is a village inside the City of Ottawa, where the Rideau River splits around Long Island. Moss Kent Dickinson built his mills here in 1860 and laid out the village around them; the name he chose comes from an Anishinaabe word meaning island in the river. What grew from the mill is now the hub of a whole group of communities — Long Island and Nicholls Island, Mahogany, Manotick East, Manotick South, North Gower, Kars and the Rideau Township countryside. The housing runs from 19th-century village homes to some of the largest custom estates in the city's south end, and this roundup's hub count reads across the village, the islands and the southern shelf together.

Getting around from Manotick

Manotick runs on cars and river roads, and it is honest about it. Highway 416 puts the village minutes from the rest of Ottawa's south end, Manotick Main Street carries the village core, and the bridges to Long Island make the island streets part of daily life rather than a detour. Dickinson Square — Watson's Mill, Dickinson House and the Carriage Shed across from the dam — anchors the core, and from the square the Main Street runs its boutique course: independent shops, cafés and restaurants in heritage storefronts, the Manotick Mews shopping enclave, and the clock tower over the village grocery.

Watson's Mill, the 1860 limestone grist and flour mill at Dickinson Square — the building the village grew around.

Schools around Manotick

School questions in Manotick turn on which side of the river — and which register — a home sits in:

  • On Long Island: St. Leonard Catholic School stands on Long Island Road, giving the island its own elementary school — a rare thing for a waterfront pocket, and part of why island families put down roots.

  • Village and mainland: the village core, Manotick Estates and the southern shelf are served by Ottawa's public and Catholic boards — but the island, village and estate pockets do not share one catchment map, so the exact street decides.

  • French boards: French-language schooling runs through the region's French public and French Catholic boards, each with its own locator and its own lines on the map.

Catchments turn on the exact street here, so check yours on each board's school-locator tool before you buy. My Manotick guide points you to the same locators.

Parks, play and daily life

David Bartlett Park gives Long Island its green centre, with river access and the dog park; Dickinson Square hosts the village's festivals around the mill; and daily life runs along Manotick Main Street — independent shops, cafés and patios in summer, a farmers' market in season, and the everyday errands covered in the core. The river is the recreation: docks, launches and sunset water are the backyard for the island streets, and the countryside districts begin where the village streets end.

Something interesting about Manotick

Dickinson Days, the village festival held on the first weekend of June around Dickinson's birthday, shuts the core down for a parade, a market and fireworks — the village celebrating the man who built it. A community that still throws a birthday party for its founder, more than a century and a half after he opened his mill, is telling you something about how it keeps its history: not in a plaque, but in the calendar.
