Schools & Catchments

Four school boards serve Barrhaven: the Ottawa-Carleton District School Board (English public), the Ottawa Catholic School Board (English Catholic), the Conseil des écoles catholiques du Centre-Est (French Catholic) and the Conseil des écoles publiques de l’Est de l’Ontario (French public). Secondary schools across the district include John McCrae, Longfields-Davidson Heights, St. Joseph Catholic, St. Mother Teresa and École secondaire catholique Pierre-Savard. Catchments follow street-level boundaries, so confirm your exact catchment in the OCDSB school locator and the equivalent finders on the other three boards’ sites before you sign a lease — a condo two blocks over can fall in a different catchment.

Commute & Transportation

Condo renters in Barrhaven get the same commute story as buyers: the Southwest Transitway runs through the district with stations at Fallowfield, Longfields, Strandherd, Marketplace, Barrhaven Centre, Beatrice and Nepean Woods; Fallowfield station adds a Park & Ride of about 1,095 spaces beside the VIA Rail Fallowfield station. By road, Highway 416 forms the western edge and the off-peak drive downtown typically runs 25 to 30 minutes. The planned Barrhaven LRT extension (Baseline Station to Barrhaven Town Centre) has a completed environmental assessment but is not yet funded — rent near the Transitway for the commute you have today, not the train you might get.

Parks, Trails & Recreation

The Jock River is Barrhaven’s defining natural feature, winding through the south end past Hearts Desire, Chapman Mills and Half Moon Bay, with the Rideau River forming the eastern boundary. The Chapman Mills Conservation Area offers trails and wildlife viewing, and neighbourhood parks, splash pads and sports fields are distributed across all eleven sub-areas — each sub-area guide names its local parks. The Walter Baker Sports Centre at 100 Malvern Drive remains the district’s recreation anchor: pool, arenas, squash and fitness, open to the public.

Shopping, Groceries & Restaurants

Barrhaven works as a “city within a city” for renters too: Chapman Mills Marketplace at 50 Marketplace Avenue (RioCan, 66 stores, Loblaws-anchored) with a Cineplex, Barrhaven Town Centre, CitiGate Marketplace and the Strandherd Road plazas cover groceries, big-box, dining and services — Farm Boy, Metro, Sobeys, Canadian Tire, Best Buy and Home Depot in the mix. Barrhaven Condo Rental Stock & What It Rents Like

Barrhaven’s condo-rental stock falls into three shapes. Apartment-style condos — low-rise and mid-rise buildings with hallways, elevators and shared amenities — cluster in Pheasant Run’s five Minto condo communities, Knollsbrook’s South Station building at 3684 Fallowfield Road and the town-centre corridor. Stacked townhomes — two-storey units stacked over each other, each with its own front door — are the growing format, with Caivan’s Conservancy and Mattamy’s stacked towns selling new in Half Moon Bay. Condo townhouses — ground-level, yard-free, often with a garage — appear as resales across the 1990s–2000s pockets. Most Barrhaven condo rentals are 2 bedroom units; 1 bedroom and 3 bedroom options exist but trade thinner.

The honest pros and cons of renting a condo in Barrhaven

Best parts: Lock-and-leave living with no yard work; shared amenities (gyms, party rooms, guest suites) bundled into the rent; underground parking standard in most buildings; and the widest range of 1-to-3 bedroom rental layouts of any Ottawa suburb. Be aware of: Condo fees are baked into your rent — you pay for amenities whether you use them or not; condo boards set the rules (pets, barbecues, move-in fees); and the thinnest pockets (Knollsbrook, On the Green, Hearts Desire) can go months with zero active condo rentals.

What types of condo rentals are available across Barrhaven’s 11 sub-areas? Three shapes: apartment-style condos (hallways, elevators, shared amenities — Pheasant Run’s Minto communities, Knollsbrook’s South Station, the town centre), stacked townhomes (own front door, two storeys — growing fast in Half Moon Bay), and condo townhouses (ground-level, garage, no yard work — resale stock across the 1990s–2000s pockets).

What is the difference between a condo apartment, a stacked townhome and a condo townhome? A condo apartment is one unit in a hallway building with shared amenities and elevators. A stacked townhome is a two-storey unit with its own front door, stacked above or below another unit — more privacy, usually no elevator. A condo townhouse is a ground-level row unit, often with a garage and small patio, in a condo corporation that handles exterior maintenance.

Do condo rentals in Barrhaven include parking and utilities? Usually parking — underground spots are standard in Barrhaven’s condo buildings, sometimes with a second spot or visitor parking. Utilities vary: many Barrhaven condo rentals include heat and water with hydro extra, but it is unit-by-unit. I confirm inclusions in writing before you apply, because “includes utilities” means different things in different buildings.

What should I check before signing a condo lease in Ontario? Use the Ontario standard lease form — it’s the law for most residential tenancies. Confirm the condo corporation’s rules on pets, smoking, barbecues and move-in/move-out fees (they bind tenants too), check who pays for what, and get the rent, term and inclusions in writing. I review every lease my renter clients sign before they commit.
