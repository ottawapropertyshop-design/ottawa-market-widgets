Condos for sale in Longfields, Barrhaven

Longfields (AMP area 7706) is Barrhaven's big 1990s–2000s residential belt — 4.6 square kilometres between Greenbank Road, Woodroffe Avenue, Fallowfield Road and Strandherd Drive, home to nearly 20,000 people — and it carries the busiest condo book in the district. Sixteen condos were active here on October 2, 2026, averaging $376,137 asking: 1-bedroom apartments observed near $299,999 (500 Claridge Drive) and $338,500 (615 Longfields Drive), 2-bedroom units at Tayside Private near $398,800–$399,900, and 3-bedroom units near $489,000. It is the rare Barrhaven pocket that gives its name to both a Transitway station and a secondary school — Longfields Station (opened spring 2011) and Longfields-Davidson Heights Secondary. Condo fees in Longfields

Longfields is where the fee benchmarks get concrete: $459 a month observed at 831 Longfields Drive (Campanale-managed). Against Ottawa 2026 norms — roughly $0.50–$0.90 per square foot per month, typical Barrhaven bills $450–$650 a month — that sits squarely in the healthy middle. Apply the cheap-fee-versus-healthy-fee check on every building: is the reserve funded toward 80%+, are increases steady year to year, and what exactly does the fee include? A slightly higher fee with a healthy reserve beats a low fee with a starved reserve — every time.

Commute and transportation from Longfields

Longfields is the only Barrhaven pocket with its own named Transitway station: Longfields Station, opened spring 2011, served by routes 75, 173, 275, 279 and 305 — Route 75 (Cambrian to Tunney's Pasture) runs 24/7. For drivers, Woodroffe Avenue and Strandherd Drive are the arterials; downtown Ottawa is 25–30 minutes off-peak. Few Barrhaven pockets make car-free commuting this practical — a real edge for condo buyers who work downtown.

Entertainment and leisure near Longfields

No cinema, bowling or arcade inside Longfields itself — the Cineplex Odeon Barrhaven is the nearest. Stated plainly: the pocket's leisure anchors are its parks, the Transitway access and Village Square's dining row.

Safety and street character in Longfields

A mature 1990s belt with high owner-occupancy (82%) and quiet interior streets. For condo buyers, the screen that matters is the corporation's financials and management — reviewed in the status-certificate check before every offer.

Future development in Longfields

Longfields itself is largely built out, but its edges are not: published plans reference a large employment zone south of Longfields and the planned Barrhaven LRT conversion of the Southwest Transitway (environmental assessment complete, not funded — long-range, not a commute you can count on).
