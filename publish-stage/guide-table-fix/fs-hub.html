History of Barrhaven

Modern Barrhaven begins with Mel Barr — Melville Barr — who bought 200 acres near Fallowfield and Greenbank roads in the 1950s intending to build a horse-racing track. When the Rideau-Carleton Raceway was built further east instead, Barr developed his land for housing, and the suburb that took his name was established in 1968. Before that the land was farm country — much of it owned by Irish Catholic families — crossed by an Indigenous route roughly followed today by Jockvale Road; a wooden schoolhouse stood near Jockvale and Strandherd roads as early as 1841, and its 1906 brick replacement still stands. Barrhaven was part of the City of Nepean until Ottawa's amalgamation in 2001. Growth was steady but services lagged for decades — one grocery store, no cinema — until the Walter Baker Sports Centre opened in 1980 and Barrhaven Town Centre opened with a range of retailers in 1991. The 21st century brought the real boom: from about 42,000 residents in 2001 to more than 100,000 twenty years later. (Sources: Wikipedia — Barrhaven.)

Shopping and dining across Barrhaven

One reason Barrhaven works as a "city within a city" is that residents rarely need to leave it to shop. Barrhaven Town Centre, opened in 1991, was the original retail core. Today the heavyweight is Chapman Mills Marketplace at 50 Marketplace Avenue: a RioCan-owned district of 66 stores and services anchored by Loblaws, with Winners, Walmart, a Cineplex cinema and the Marketplace Transitway station on site. CitiGate Marketplace, the Strandherd Road plazas, Farm Boy, Metro, Sobeys, Canadian Tire, Best Buy and Home Depot round out the big-box layer. Dining runs from chains — East Side Mario's, Boston Pizza, Montana's, Lone Star Texas Grill, Kelsey's, Swiss Chalet — to well-loved independents like the Barley Mow and local shawarma and sushi rooms. The Barrhaven BIA supports local businesses and community programming across the district.

Transportation and commuting

Barrhaven's commute story has two halves: the bus network residents use today and the rail line planned for tomorrow. The Southwest Transitway runs through Barrhaven with stations at Fallowfield, Longfields, Strandherd, Marketplace, Barrhaven Centre, Beatrice and Nepean Woods. Fallowfield station, at Woodroffe Avenue and Fallowfield Road, is the main hub: a Park & Ride lot of about 1,095 spaces beside the VIA Rail Fallowfield station, which puts intercity rail within the suburb. By road, Highway 416 forms Barrhaven's western edge, and Strandherd Drive, Greenbank Road, Woodroffe Avenue and Fallowfield Road are the main arterials; an off-peak drive to downtown Ottawa typically runs 25 to 30 minutes. Looking ahead, the City of Ottawa has completed the environmental assessment for a Barrhaven LRT extension from Baseline Station to Barrhaven Town Centre — a planned future line, not yet under construction, so treat any timeline you read online with caution and check the City's current project page.

O-Train LRT — where Barrhaven fits in the network

The rail half of that story is the planned Barrhaven LRT: the City has completed the environmental assessment for a roughly 10 km extension from Baseline Station to Barrhaven Town Centre that would convert the existing Southwest Transitway — including Fallowfield, Longfields, Strandherd and Barrhaven Centre stations — from bus rapid transit to light rail, and add three new stations (Tallwood, Knoxdale, Nepean Sportsplex) north of the Transitway. Status as of October 2026: the environmental assessment is complete, but the project is neither approved nor funded — so it belongs in the long-range column, not in the commute you can count on today.

Elsewhere on the network, as of October 2026: Line 1 (Confederation) runs Blair to Tunney’s Pasture, with its east extension to Trim in final testing and expected to open late 2026 and its west extension to Algonquin planned for 2027; Line 2 (Trillium) and the Line 4 airport spur have been running since January 2025; and a future Line 3 will run Trim to Moodie, sharing Line 1’s tracks east of Lincoln Fields. Four school boards serve Barrhaven: the Ottawa-Carleton District School Board (English public), the Ottawa Catholic School Board (English Catholic), the Conseil des écoles catholiques du Centre-Est (French Catholic) and the Conseil des écoles publiques de l'Est de l'Ontario (French public). Parks, trails and the Jock River

The Jock River is Barrhaven's defining natural feature, winding through the south end past Hearts Desire, Chapman Mills and Half Moon Bay, while the Rideau River forms the suburb's eastern boundary. The Chapman Mills Conservation Area offers trails and wildlife viewing along the Jock, and the City has continued extending the riverside pathway network. Neighbourhood parks, splash pads and sports fields are distributed across all eleven sub-areas — each sub-area guide names its local parks specifically.

Entertainment and leisure

Before 2005, Barrhaven had no cinema at all; today the Cineplex Odeon Barrhaven at 131 Riocan Avenue (opened October 2005) includes an XSCAPE entertainment centre. The Walter Baker Sports Centre at 100 Malvern Drive remains the recreation anchor for the whole district: swimming, arenas, squash courts and fitness facilities open to the public. Evening and weekend life otherwise centres on the Marketplace district's restaurants and the community programming run through the Barrhaven BIA and local community associations.

Healthcare and services

Day-to-day services are distributed across the retail districts rather than concentrated in one medical precinct: family dental and orthodontic practices, walk-in and family clinics, physiotherapy and chiropractic clinics, opticians, pharmacies (including Shoppers Drug Mart locations) and banks sit within Barrhaven Town Centre, Chapman Mills Marketplace and the Strandherd plazas. For hospital care, residents use the Queensway Carleton Hospital to the northwest or CHEO and the Ottawa Hospital campuses closer to the core. Buying and financing in Barrhaven in 2026

Mortgage default insurance is required in Canada whenever the down payment is under 20% of the purchase price — it protects the lender, not the buyer, and is provided by CMHC, Sagen or Canada Guaranty. The minimum down payment starts at 5%: 5% on the first $500,000 of the price and 10% on the portion above $500,000, with insured financing available on homes priced below $1.5 million (rules and caps change — confirm the current version on the CMHC mortgage loan insurance explainer before you budget).

Offer strategy in 2026 looks different from the bidding-war years. With Barrhaven averaging 33 days on market in the twelve months to April 2026, conditions on financing and home inspection are normal and widely accepted — use them. A typical deposit with an accepted offer is about 5%, held in trust by the listing brokerage. On condominiums, always order the status certificate and have it reviewed before you firm up. Get pre-approved before you start shopping, and bring me the number — I will tell you honestly which of the eleven pockets it buys into. Buying new construction? The Ontario enhanced HST rebate (up to $130,000, ending March 31, 2027) can change the math — confirm the fine print against CRA Notice 346 before you count on it.

03 — Barrhaven Neighbourhoods

Is Barrhaven a good place to live in Ottawa?

Barrhaven suits buyers who want a full suburban package — schools from all four boards, the Marketplace and Town Centre retail districts, the Transitway, the Jock River trail system and the Walter Baker Sports Centre — without leaving the suburb for daily life. The trade-off is distance: it sits about 17 km southwest of downtown, so commuters should weigh the 25–30 minute off-peak drive or the Transitway ride.

Which Barrhaven neighbourhood is the best?

It depends on your budget and priorities. Stonebridge and Hearts Desire top the price table for executive homes and river/golf settings; Pheasant Run, Knollsbrook and Cedargrove and Fraserdale offer established, more affordable streets; Half Moon Bay has the newest construction and the most annual sales. How is the commute from Barrhaven to downtown Ottawa?

By car outside rush hour, typically 25 to 30 minutes via Highway 416 and the Queensway. By transit, the Southwest Transitway (Fallowfield, Longfields, Strandherd, Marketplace, Barrhaven Centre, Beatrice, Nepean Woods stations) connects through to downtown; Fallowfield's Park & Ride holds about 1,095 cars. A future Barrhaven LRT extension is planned but not yet under construction.

Are there good schools in Barrhaven?

Yes — schools from all four Ottawa boards, including John McCrae, Longfields-Davidson Heights, St. Joseph Catholic, St. Mother Teresa and École secondaire catholique Pierre-Savard at the secondary level.
