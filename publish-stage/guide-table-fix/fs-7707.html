Hearts Desire is Barrhaven’s prestige pocket: a small, low-density enclave wrapped by Jockvale Road and Prince of Wales Drive to the west, Woodroffe Avenue to the east, Strandherd Drive to the north and the Jock River to the south. The homes are detached, custom, and spread across oversized tree-covered lots — many around half an acre — built from the 1970s through the early 2000s. There are no townhomes and no semis here; the low-density character is protected, and it shows.

The trade is deliberate: you give up a Transitway station of your own and the newest floor plans, and you get space, privacy, mature maples and a river at the end of the street — inside a suburb that otherwise grew up around you. Everything Barrhaven offers day to day — the Walter Baker Sports Centre, the Strandherd shopping corridor, four school boards — sits minutes away, while the enclave itself stays country-quiet.

Commute and Transit from Hearts Desire, Ottawa

Hearts Desire is a driver’s enclave, honestly stated. Downtown is roughly 20 minutes off-peak: Greenbank Road or Prince of Wales Drive north to the core, or Strandherd Drive west to Highway 416 for Kanata and the 417. The airport runs about 20 minutes via Prince of Wales Drive. There is no Transitway station inside the enclave — the nearest are Strandherd Station and Barrhaven Centre Station on the Transitway, a short drive or local-bus ride away, with OC Transpo routes running along Greenbank Road, Strandherd Drive and Prince of Wales Drive. Most households here simply plan around two cars.

Parks, the Jock River and Recreation in Hearts Desire

The green anchor is Heart’s Desire Park (3566 Woodroffe Avenue) — roughly 32 acres of mature maple forest with walking trails, an off-leash dog park, and the Jock River along its edge. The river itself is the enclave’s south boundary: a 72 km tributary of the Rideau, with a paddle launch (Jock River Landing) just across Prince of Wales Drive. Smaller parks fill the pockets — Barcham Park (80 Bren-Maur Road) and Davidson Park (5 Ryerson Avenue, sports field and lit tennis courts) — and the Walter Baker Sports Centre on Malvern Drive, with its pools, arenas and library branch, is minutes north. Jockvale Road itself follows an old Indigenous route west of the river; an 1841 schoolhouse once stood at Jockvale and Strandherd.

It is the standard Barrhaven errand run, reached without crossing the suburb.

Entertainment and Things to Do near Hearts Desire

Housing Stock in Hearts Desire — Estate Detached Homes Only

One housing form defines 7707: the detached estate home. Expect two-storeys, bungalows and sidesplits from the 1970s to the early 2000s, most of them custom or one-off builds rather than production models, on lots that dwarf modern Barrhaven — some approaching half an acre, a few with Jock River frontage. Because some properties predate full municipal servicing on their street, a share of homes run on a private well and septic system — a normal, inspectable part of buying here, covered in the questions below. There are no townhomes, no semis and no condos in the enclave stock; if you want attached living in south Barrhaven, Longfields and Half Moon Bay are the pockets to read.

Safety and Character in Hearts Desire

Hearts Desire — the honest pros and cons

Pros: the largest, quietest lots in Barrhaven; mature trees and a real forest park on a river; custom homes with character instead of production sameness; a prestige address that holds value through scarcity — 17 sales a year means owners, not flippers; Strandherd shopping and Highway 416 minutes away despite the country feel.

Cons: the highest entry price of any pocket in this series — today’s floor is a $674,880 vacant lot; almost no choice at any given moment (4 listings today, sometimes zero); no Transitway station of your own and a two-car reality; some homes on private well and septic, with the inspection and documentation that requires; dated interiors are common at the lower end — budget the renovation.

Who should move to Hearts Desire?

Move-up buyers leaving production Barrhaven who want the lot and the quiet without leaving the suburb — this is the step up that stays in Barrhaven. Custom-build buyers who would rather build one estate home on a mature treed street than in a raw new subdivision. Long-horizon owners who buy once and stay — the enclave rewards patience and punishes speculation. If you need a station at the end of the street or a sub-$700K detached, Hearts Desire is the wrong pocket — read the Longfields guide instead; if you want the trees, the river and the room, nothing else in Barrhaven is this.
