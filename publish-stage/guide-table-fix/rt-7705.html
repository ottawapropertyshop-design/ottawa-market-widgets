The pocket — 1980s freehold streets in mid-Barrhaven

On the Green (7705) is the established pocket bounded by Jockvale Road to the west, Greenbank Road to the east, Strandherd Drive to the south and the rail tracks below Fallowfield Road to the north. The streets — Bartley Crescent, Exeter Drive, Benlark Road, Wessex Road, Fulham Court E, Atoll Street, Avery Crescent, Chester Crescent, Danaher Drive, Starling Crescent and their neighbours, with Tartan Drive running through the west side — were built out in the 1980s: detached two-storeys and freehold townhomes, many renovated, on honest family lots. There is no condo stock inside the pocket and essentially no new construction; change here comes from infill and renovation, not subdivisions. That is why rental supply is sporadic — homes turn over one at a time, when an owner moves, is posted away, or decides to hold and lease.

Who rents in On the Green — and why listings go fast

Three renter profiles land here on purpose. School-first families who want the Jockvale Elementary → Cedarview Middle → John McCrae Secondary chain — or St. Joseph High School on Greenbank — without buying in. Relocating households and professionals on postings who prefer an established, central mid-Barrhaven street over a brand-new subdivision rental. And renters priced out of the Stonebridge and Half Moon Bay purchase pockets who still want Greenbank–Strandherd access and mature streets. Because the pocket is fully built out and owner-dominated, a well-kept 3- or 4-bedroom detached or a freehold townhome here draws applications quickly — being ready before one lists is the whole game, and the live map above is the watch tool.

Schools serving On the Green renters

No school building sits inside the pocket; the serving schools ring it, and the public chain is confirmed by the schools themselves: Jockvale Elementary School (101 Malvern Drive, K–6, English and early French immersion) feeds Cedarview Middle School (2760 Cedarview Road, grades 7–8), which feeds John McCrae Secondary School (103 Malvern Drive, grades 9–12). Catholic families are served by St. Luke School in Nepean, St. Joseph High School (3333 Greenbank Road, grades 7–12) and St. Mother Teresa High School (440 Longfields Drive, grades 7–12) — which Catholic secondary a given address is zoned to varies by street. French Catholic options are École élémentaire catholique Jean-Robert-Gauthier (651 Chapman Mills Drive) and École secondaire catholique Pierre-Savard (1110 Longfields Drive); French public options are École élémentaire publique Michaëlle-Jean (11 Claridge Drive, IB Primary Years) and École secondaire publique Pierre-de-Blois (1310 Chapman Mills Drive, IB Middle Years). School zones shift and are set by address — confirm yours with the board's school locator before you sign, and tell me the school first: I will confirm the catchment for any specific address.

Child care in and around the pocket

Educara Montessori Child Care sits inside the pocket at 275 Tartan Drive — licensed, part of the CWELCC program, with infant, toddler and preschool spaces. On the Jockvale Road edge, Guide and Grow Daycare Centre runs preschool (3013 Jockvale) and toddler (3058 Jockvale) programs, also licensed and CWELCC. For school-age care, the OCDSB Extended Day Program at Jockvale Elementary (101 Malvern Drive) covers before- and after-school hours right at the neighbourhood school.

Churches and community

Sequoia Community Church (255 Tartan Drive) worships inside the pocket. On the Jockvale Road edge sit Barrhaven United Church (3013 Jockvale) and Barrhaven Fellowship Christian Reformed Church (3058 Jockvale). The West Barrhaven Community Association covers the pocket — it manages the Charlie Conacher Community Building at 30 Wessex Drive and runs the Moloughney Park outdoor rink in winter, the kind of volunteer backbone that makes an established pocket feel settled rather than anonymous.

Parks, recreation and the namesake

Moloughney Park — also signed as Wessex Park, at 30 Wessex Road — is the pocket's own green: ball diamond, soccer fields, basketball court, play structures, the Charlie Conacher Community Building and the winter outdoor rink. Malvern Park (100 Malvern Drive, about seven acres of playgrounds and trails) sits just north across the tracks, and Greenbank Park (851 Greenbank Road) adds a splash pad on the east edge. The recreation anchor is Walter Baker Sports Centre (100 Malvern Drive, pictured above): two ice rinks, a 25-metre 8-lane lap pool with dive tank and teaching pools, a rock wall, gyms and squash courts — plus the Ruth E. Dickinson branch of the Ottawa Public Library under the same roof. And the namesake: On the Green Driving Range & Mini Golf (3232 Jockvale Road), the family-run range the pocket is named for, confirmed operating in 2026 — the range is the draw; the mini-putt is cheerfully weathered.

Transit and commuting from On the Green

OC Transpo Route 70 runs the pocket's own streets — Tartan Drive, Exeter Drive and Wessex Drive — to Fallowfield Station, so most addresses here have a bus on their own street, not just at the boundary. Route 75 gives frequent service along the Greenbank Road corridor on the east edge, and Route 173 serves the Malvern Drive stop area by Walter Baker and John McCrae. Strandherd Park & Ride (3680 Strandherd Drive, 300+ spaces) is just south of the pocket, and Fallowfield Station — Transitway, park & ride and the VIA Rail station — is just north. By car, Greenbank and Strandherd arterials and Highway 416 access put downtown at roughly 25–30 minutes off-peak.

Shopping and everyday life

The pocket itself is purely residential, which keeps it quiet — and nothing is far. Directly across Strandherd Drive sits the plaza with Walmart, Loblaws, Staples, Winners and Sport Chek. The Barrhaven Town Centre and Marketplace / Chapman Mills corridors are minutes away by car or bus for everything else, from groceries to the cinema.

What Ottawa landlords ask for — come prepared

Ontario landlords typically ask for a credit check, proof of income or an employment letter, references from previous landlords, and first and last month's rent as deposit — the only legal deposit in Ontario; "key money" is not permitted. Ontario's Standard Form of Lease is mandatory for most tenancies. Have your documents ready before you tour: in a pocket like On the Green, where a listing is an event, the prepared applicant wins — and I can get your application in front of the listing agent the same day.

Ontario renting essentials

Most leases start with a one-year term, then continue month-to-month. Rent control applies to units first occupied before November 15, 2018. "No pets" clauses are void under Ontario's Residential Tenancies Act, which makes freehold houses the pet-friendly choice. Utilities are often tenant-paid; snow removal and lawn care are negotiable.
