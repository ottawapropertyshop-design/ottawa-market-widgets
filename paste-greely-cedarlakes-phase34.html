The Cedarlakes Phase 3 and 4 proposal in Greely — 71 estate lots south of Cedarlakes Way

The existing Cedarlakes community was built in two phases: Plan 4M-1479 (Lots 1 to 40) and Plan 4M-1555 (Lots 1 to 20) — 60 lots on Cedarlakes Way and the south end of Spartan Grove, in Greely’s southwest corner. The new proposal is the third and fourth phase of the same Greely subdivision. The phase names come from the applicant’s own technical studies — the transportation, servicing and geotechnical reports filed with the City are all titled for Cedar Lakes Phase 3 and 4 — while the City’s application files describe the proposal plainly as a 71-lot subdivision at Cedar Lake in Greely, at 1600 Stagecoach Road.

What the draft plan proposes, from the City of Ottawa application files for this Greely proposal (subdivision file D07-16-24-0001 and zoning file D02-02-24-0052):

  • 71 residential lots, combined across Phase 3 and Phase 4. The applications treat the two phases as one draft plan and do not publish a lot count for each Greely phase separately.

  • Estate lots: every lot a minimum of 0.4 hectares (about one acre) with a minimum width of 30 metres, for single-detached homes — the same estate-lot formula as the built Cedarlakes streets elsewhere in Greely.

  • Private services: each home on its own private well and septic system. There are no municipal water or sewer services to these Greely lands — the same private-servicing picture as most of rural Greely.

  • The land: about 41.4 hectares of vacant, partially treed Greely land, with about 198 metres of frontage on Stagecoach Road and a split frontage on Cedarlakes Way — the undeveloped blocks left inside and below the registered Phase 1 and 2 plans.

  • Four road connections: Cedarlakes Way is proposed to be extended south into the new Greely subdivision; a new road is proposed off Cedarlakes Way; a further connection is proposed from Deermeadow Drive; and a new connection is proposed to Stagecoach Road. Two new north-south roads inside the plan end in cul-de-sacs.

  • Water and green space: two new stormwater management ponds along the southern boundary of the Greely site, with an existing pond retained; park blocks in the southwest corner and along Stagecoach Road; trail blocks along the southern boundary, with public trails to the park.

71 homes — or 74? Every document in the City’s files says 71: the subdivision application, the zoning application, and the applicant’s own transportation and servicing studies all propose 71 residential lots for Phase 3 and 4 combined. No City or developer document found for this Greely guide proposes 74 homes. The draft plan does number blocks beyond the lots — Block 72 and Block 78 are parks, and Blocks 73 and 74 are trails — which may be where a higher figure gets repeated, but those blocks are parkland and trails, not houses. The residential count for this Greely proposal is 71.

Where the approvals stand for Cedarlakes Phase 3 and 4, Greely

The road to this draft plan has not been straight, and for a Greely buyer the honest position matters more than a hopeful one. The applicant’s studies for this Greely proposal were prepared through 2023. The subdivision application (D07-16-24-0001) was filed with the City in January 2024, and the companion zoning amendment (D02-02-24-0052) followed in September 2024. The lands carry a planning wrinkle: Official Plan Amendment 5 added them to the Village of Greely Secondary Plan as Village Residential in September 2023, and provincial legislation in December 2023 then removed them from the Greely village boundary in the Official Plan’s Schedule B9 — one of the reasons this file has been contested.

The City refused the subdivision application, and the applicant appealed to the Ontario Land Tribunal. The City’s development application registry now shows both Greely files closed: the subdivision file reads “Post Approval — OMB Hearing Held” (March 2026), and the zoning file reads “Application Approved in part by OMB” (March 2026). What that does not mean: no plan of subdivision has been registered for Phase 3 and 4, no internal street exists, no lot has been released, and nothing is selling. On the developer’s own website, the Cedar Lakes page does not mention the Greely Phase 3 or Phase 4 at all, and its available-lots page lists only its Forest Lake Estates community. There is no pricing and no timeline published anywhere for these Greely phases, and this guide will update the day their status genuinely changes.

How the new phases join the existing Greely community at Cedarlakes

On paper, Greely’s Phase 3 and 4 are designed as a continuation, not a separate enclave: the same developer, the same one-acre estate-lot formula on private well and septic, and road connections that tie the new streets directly into Cedarlakes Way, Deermeadow Drive and Stagecoach Road. A home built there one day would sit immediately south of the existing Cedarlakes streets, in the same lake-community setting in Greely’s southwest.

One limit, stated plainly because Greely buyers ask: the application files do not say whether future Phase 3 and 4 residents would belong to the Cedarlakes Association or share its dock and lake access on Cedar Lake. That is not promised anywhere in the proposal, so it should not be assumed. What exists and can be bought in Greely today is the built community — its resale homes are tracked, with live listings, in the Cedarlakes Greely Homes for Sale Neighbourhood Guide.

Cedarlakes Phase 3 and 4 Greely questions, answered

Are Cedarlakes Phase 3 and 4 lots for sale?
No. There is no registered plan, no lot release, no pricing and no timeline published for these Greely phases, and the developer’s own website lists no Cedar Lakes lots for sale. Anyone offering a Greely Phase 3 or 4 lot today is ahead of the record.

Is it 71 homes or 74 homes?
71 residential lots, combined across Phase 3 and Phase 4 — that is the figure in the City’s subdivision and zoning files and in the applicant’s own studies. No document found for this guide proposes 74 homes; the draft plan’s higher block numbers (Blocks 72 to 78) are parks and trails, not houses, and the applications do not split the 71 Greely lots between Phase 3 and Phase 4 separately.

Who is the developer of Cedarlakes Phase 3 and 4 in Greely?
Sunset Lakes Developments, through 6980848 Canada Corporation — the applicant named on both City files for this Greely land, and the same developer behind the built Cedarlakes phases (Plans 4M-1479 and 4M-1555), Sunset Lakes, Woodstream, South Village, Lakewood Trails and Forest Lake Estates in Greely.

How big are the Phase 3 and 4 lots, and how are they serviced?
Every proposed Greely lot is a minimum of 0.4 hectares — about one acre — with a minimum width of 30 metres, for single-detached homes. Each home would be serviced by a private well and a private septic system; there are no municipal water or sewer services to these Greely lands.

When will Greely’s Phase 3 and 4 be built?
There is no published timeline. The City’s registry shows the subdivision file closed after a tribunal hearing and the zoning approved in part by the tribunal in March 2026, but a tribunal outcome is not a registration, a servicing program or a sales launch. Which schools would serve Cedarlakes Phase 3 and 4 in Greely?
The designated paths checked in the boards’ locators for the Greely phase lands are set out in the schools section above — the same Greely schools as the built Cedarlakes streets, from Greely Elementary to Osgoode Township High School on the public side. Will Greely’s Phase 3 and 4 homes have access to Cedar Lake?
The application files do not say. The Cedarlakes Association runs the dock and lake access for the existing Greely community, and nothing in the Phase 3 and 4 proposal promises that future residents join it. Treat lake access as unconfirmed until the developer states it in writing.
