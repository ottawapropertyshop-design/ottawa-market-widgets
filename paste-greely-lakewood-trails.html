The community — Plan 4M-1636, Sunset Lakes Developments’ lake community

Lakewood Trails is community number seven from Sunset Lakes Developments — the builder behind Greely’s original Sunset Lakes — registered as Plan 4M-1636 and launched in May 2017. Where the earlier Sunset Lakes communities were built around spring-fed lakes in Greely’s northwest, Lakewood Trails brought the same idea to the village’s east side: a compact estate enclave wrapped around two private lakes, with the homes of Shoreway Drive holding the waterfront lots.

Three streets carry the whole community. Shoreway Drive is the marquee street — the waterfront crescent along the lakes, reached by Donwell Drive and Center Lake Way. Center Lake Way is the connector into the pocket, and Lake Trail Road serves the community’s east side. Because the plan is small and fully built around the lakes, there is no through traffic: the streets belong to the residents, the lakes are private, and the whole enclave reads as one finished piece rather than a subdivision in progress.

Photo: Tomasz R. Witek — the Residents’ Club grounds at Lakewood Trails.

The Residents’ Club is the centre of daily life. The community building, outdoor pool and fitness centre give Lakewood Trails something most Greely enclaves do not have: a shared amenity core inside the neighbourhood itself. In summer the pool and the lakes carry the community’s social life; the rest of the year, the club is where the neighbourhood gathers. When you compare a Lakewood Trails resale against a similar estate home elsewhere in Greely, that club — and the private-lake frontage on Shoreway Drive — is the difference you are paying for.

Living in Lakewood Trails

Daily life here starts with the water: two private lakes inside the plan, waterfront lots on Shoreway Drive, and the Residents’ Club — community building, outdoor pool and fitness centre — as the neighbourhood’s gathering place. The village core is about 1.4 km north — The Shoppes of Greely plaza on Bank Street for groceries and everyday services, the Greely Community Centre and the library branch on Meadow Drive. Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31) is the spine north: downtown Ottawa is roughly half an hour in good traffic. The honest transit picture matches the rest of Greely: no regular OC Transpo route runs through the village — the Thursday Shopper Route 304 is the one exception — so Lakewood Trails is a driver’s address, and school buses, run by the Ottawa Student Transportation Authority, are the transit that matters to families here.

Lakewood Trails questions, answered

What is Lakewood Trails?
A waterfront estate community in Greely’s east side — Plan 4M-1636, community number seven from Sunset Lakes Developments, launched in May 2017. It is built around two private lakes on three streets: Shoreway Drive, Center Lake Way and Lake Trail Road, with a Residents’ Club at its centre.

What is the Residents’ Club?
The community’s own amenity core: a community building with an outdoor pool and a fitness centre, reserved for Lakewood Trails residents. Together with the two private lakes, it is what separates Lakewood Trails from Greely’s other estate enclaves.

Which schools serve Lakewood Trails?
The designated paths are set out in the schools section above, confirmed in the boards’ own locators in November 2026: Greely Elementary and Castor Valley on the public side, Osgoode Township High School for Grades 9 to 12, St. Mary to St. Mark on the Catholic side, Michel-Dupuis to Pierre-de-Blois in French public, and Jonathan-Pitre to Pierre-Savard in French Catholic. Confirm any exact address in the board’s school locator before you buy.

Is Lakewood Trails the same as Sunset Lakes?
No — they are sister communities from the same developer, Sunset Lakes Developments, on opposite sides of the village. Sunset Lakes is the original lake community in Greely’s northwest; Lakewood Trails is the later, smaller enclave in the east, launched in 2017. Each has its own guide in the series table below.
