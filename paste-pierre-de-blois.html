Pierre-de-Blois Secondary School — The Facts

Pierre-de-Blois is Ottawa’s newest French-language public secondary school. It opened in September 2021 on Chapman Mills Drive in Barrhaven — a purpose-built school for the fast-growing south-west — and it teaches entirely in French: for many families that full francophone environment is the point of the move.

A note on program tracks. Pierre-de-Blois offers the International Baccalaureate program — a stated part of the school’s offer. Programs like these are exactly why the board runs an open-choice policy at the secondary level, explained in the pathway section below: living inside the sector designates Pierre-de-Blois as your teenager’s French public high school; program places are confirmed with the school.

The School Pathway Into Pierre-de-Blois

Pierre-de-Blois is a Grades 7 to 12 school, so the French public path through the south-west is short and clean: a CEPEO elementary school for Kindergarten to Grade 6, then straight into Pierre-de-Blois for the full secondary run — no separate middle school in between. Inside this sector, the board’s own locator designates École élémentaire publique Michaëlle-Jean for Barrhaven addresses, and École élémentaire publique Michel-Dupuis for the Greely and Riverside South side. The designated path for an address inside this catchment reads as one clear line:

CEPEO elementary school, Kindergarten to Grade 6 → École secondaire publique Pierre-de-Blois, Grades 7 to 12.

That single line is why the sector matters at purchase time. A family buying inside it is not buying six years of high school — it is buying the whole French public run from Kindergarten to graduation on one board, without a change of school board in between. Confirm the elementary designation for your exact address, because the elementary lines inside the sector are drawn school by school.

One Ottawa rule worth knowing. The CEPEO also publishes an open-choice policy for Ottawa secondary students: to give every student fair access to specialized secondary programs, a student who lives in the Ottawa region may attend the Ottawa CEPEO secondary school of their choice, and the board provides a free OC Transpo PRESTO pass for the trip. The sector on this page remains your designated school — the address guarantee behind this guide — and the choice policy is the board’s own addition on top of it. The school itself, and the transport consortium’s BusPlanner check, settle any individual case.

The Fraser Rating — One Measure, In Context

The Fraser Institute rated Pierre-de-Blois 6.4 out of 10 in its 2025 Report Card on Ontario secondary schools — 326th of 747 schools. Inside this series, read it in its own company: it is the only French-language public secondary serving Ottawa’s south-west, it opened only in 2021, and its number reflects a full French-language program — including the International Baccalaureate — taught to every student in the sector, not a selected intake.

Use the rating the way it deserves to be used: as a measure of past academic results at the school, not a promise about any one child. What the number cannot show is the francophone environment itself, the teachers your teenager will actually get, or the walk down Chapman Mills Drive every morning. Visit the school, walk the streets around it, and let the rating be one input — a fair one — among several.

Pierre-de-Blois or Gisèle-Lalonde? The French Public Choice

Ottawa’s French public map has two secondaries in this series. East of the city, Gisèle-Lalonde serves Orléans from boulevard Millennium; here in the south-west, Pierre-de-Blois serves Barrhaven and everything south and east of it toward Russell. The practical difference for a buyer is simple: one board, two French public high schools, and a sector line running between them. If the school is the reason for the move, the address check comes before the offer — every time.

There are also English-board mirrors to know: the same Barrhaven streets sit inside the John McCrae and Longfields-Davidson Heights stories for the Ottawa-Carleton board, and Barrhaven’s French Catholic secondary is Pierre-Savard.
