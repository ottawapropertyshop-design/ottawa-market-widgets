The Catchment, Street by Street

Read the sector the way Orléans reads itself. The north edge runs along the Ottawa River and Highway 174, taking in Convent Glen and Châteauneuf. The west edge follows the Jeanne d’Arc Boulevard line and the older Convent Glen South streets. The south reach runs past Innes Road into Avalon and Notting Gate — the growth side of Orléans — and the sector’s eastern edge leaves the subdivisions behind and runs into rural Ottawa and beyond: on the board’s map this is one of the largest school sectors in the city, because it also carries the French public families of the eastern countryside toward Clarence-Rockland.

Inside the line sit the streets families ask about by name: the established crescents of Convent Glen, the family streets of Avalon and Avalon West around the school itself, and the newer builds of Notting Gate. That mix is the catchment’s character in one paragraph: mature trees and 1970s family homes at the north end, a deep bench of newer detached and townhouse product through the middle, and rural properties on large lots at the sector’s eastern reach.

Gisèle-Lalonde Secondary School — The Facts

Gisèle-Lalonde is a French-language public secondary school of roughly a thousand students in Orléans, home of the Titans. It opened in 2003 — the board’s flagship Orléans secondary — and it teaches entirely in French: for many families that full francophone environment, in the middle of the most francophone part of Ottawa, is the point of the move. The school day runs 9:10 AM to 3:30 PM.

A note on program tracks. Gisèle-Lalonde is one of the CEPEO’s International Baccalaureate schools — its IB Middle Years and Diploma programmes are a stated part of the school’s offer — and it runs Specialist High Skills Majors in arts and culture and in sports. Programs like these are exactly why the board runs an open-choice policy at the secondary level, explained in the pathway section below: living inside the sector designates Gisèle-Lalonde as your teenager’s French public high school; program places are confirmed with the school.

The School Pathway Into Gisèle-Lalonde

Gisèle-Lalonde is a Grades 7 to 12 school, so the French public path through Orléans is short and clean: a CEPEO elementary school for Kindergarten to Grade 6, then straight into Gisèle-Lalonde for the full secondary run — no separate middle school in between. Inside this sector, the board’s own elementary sectors belong to Jeanne-Sauvé in Convent Glen South, Des Sentiers in Avalon, and L’Aventure — the board’s newest elementary school, opened in Orléans South in 2026 — while the sector’s rural reach belongs to Carrefour Jeunesse in Clarence-Rockland. The designated path for an address inside this catchment reads as one clear line:

CEPEO elementary school, Kindergarten to Grade 6 → École secondaire publique Gisèle-Lalonde, Grades 7 to 12.

That single line is why the sector matters at purchase time. A family buying inside it is not buying six years of high school — it is buying the whole French public run from Kindergarten to graduation on one board, without a change of school board in between. Confirm the elementary designation for your exact address, because the elementary lines inside Orléans are drawn school by school.

One Ottawa rule worth knowing. The CEPEO also publishes an open-choice policy for Ottawa secondary students: to give every student fair access to specialized secondary programs, a student who lives in the Ottawa region may attend the Ottawa CEPEO secondary school of their choice, and the board provides a free OC Transpo PRESTO pass for the trip. The sector on this page remains your designated school — the address guarantee behind this guide — and the choice policy is the board’s own addition on top of it. The school itself, and the transport consortium’s BusPlanner check, settle any individual case.

And one change already announced. The board is building a new French public secondary school in Orléans South — École secondaire publique La Relève, at 2405 chemin Mer Bleue, opening for the 2027-2028 school year — and its arrival will redraw this sector: the board’s consultation on the proposed new boundaries for Gisèle-Lalonde and Louis-Riel ran in November 2026, with the final decision set for February 23, 2027. Families buying in Avalon and Notting Gate for the long run should know that process is live — and it is one more reason the address check below matters before you offer.

The Fraser Rating — One Measure, In Context

The Fraser Institute rated Gisèle-Lalonde 6.5 out of 10 in its 2025 Report Card on Ontario secondary schools — 311th of 747 schools. Inside this series, read it in its own company: it is the only French-language public secondary serving Orléans, and its number reflects a full French-language program — including the International Baccalaureate — taught to every student in the sector, not a selected intake.

Use the rating the way it deserves to be used: as a measure of past academic results at the school, not a promise about any one child. What the number cannot show is the francophone environment itself, the teachers your teenager will actually get, or the walk down boulevard Millennium every morning. Visit the school, walk the streets around it, and let the rating be one input — a fair one — among several.

Gisèle-Lalonde or Louis-Riel? The Orléans Choice

West of this sector’s line, Orléans changes French public secondaries. Louis-Riel, on chemin Bearbrook in Blackburn Hamlet, serves the western side of Orléans for the CEPEO, and its guide is coming in this series. The practical difference for a buyer is simple: one suburb, two French public high schools, and a sector line running between them. If the school is the reason for the move, the address check comes before the offer — every time.

There is also an English-board mirror to know: the same eastern Orléans streets sit inside the Colonel By story for the Ottawa-Carleton board, and bilingual families often weigh the two systems street by street. The Colonel By guide in this series covers that side of the decision.

Which streets are inside the Gisèle-Lalonde catchment?
The sector covers eastern Orléans — Convent Glen and Châteauneuf along the river, Convent Glen South, Avalon and Avalon West, and Notting Gate — and reaches east into rural Ottawa toward Clarence-Rockland. The searches on this page run on the CEPEO’s own published sector, so if a home shows in the results here, its address is inside the catchment.

What is the school path for a child living in this catchment?
A CEPEO elementary school for Kindergarten to Grade 6, then École secondaire publique Gisèle-Lalonde for Grades 7 to 12 — one school for the whole secondary run, with no separate middle school. Inside the sector, the elementary sectors belong to Jeanne-Sauvé, Des Sentiers and L’Aventure, and in the rural reach to Carrefour Jeunesse.

Can an Ottawa student choose a different CEPEO secondary school?
The board publishes an open-choice policy for Ottawa: to give every student fair access to specialized secondary programs, a student living in the Ottawa region may attend the Ottawa CEPEO secondary school of their choice, and the board provides a free OC Transpo PRESTO pass. Gisèle-Lalonde remains the designated school for addresses inside this sector, and out-of-sector attendance follows the board’s published exceptions.

Is the IB program guaranteed if we live in the catchment?
Gisèle-Lalonde is one of the CEPEO’s International Baccalaureate schools, offering the Middle Years and Diploma programmes as part of the school’s program offer. How strong is Gisèle-Lalonde academically?
The Fraser Institute rated Gisèle-Lalonde 6.5 out of 10 in its 2025 Report Card on Ontario secondary schools, ranked 311th of 747. It is the only French-language public secondary school serving Orléans, and the rating is a measure of past results, best used as one input in the decision.

Will the Gisèle-Lalonde boundary change?
