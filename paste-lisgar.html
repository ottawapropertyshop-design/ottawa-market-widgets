The Catchment, Street by Street

Read the boundary the way the board drew it. The western lobe is LeBreton Flats and the downtown core — the flats’ new towers, the government blocks and the Centretown grid between Bronson Avenue and the Rideau Canal. The southern edge runs along the canal and the Queensway: everything south of that line, the Glebe included, is another school’s catchment. East of the canal the line takes in Sandy Hill — the university quarter and the embassy streets — then follows the Rideau River’s east bank through New Edinburgh and Lindenlea and runs north into Rockcliffe Park, the city’s oldest garden suburb, along the Ottawa River shore. A southern reach of Old Ottawa East, between the canal and the Rideau River, closes the loop.

Three edges are worth reading carefully, because downtown families get them wrong. Beechwood Avenue is a split street: the line runs along it, so the New Edinburgh side is in and the Vanier side is out — and Vanier and Overbrook, east of the Rideau River, are outside the catchment entirely, however close they look on the map. The Glebe is the second trap: it borders the catchment along the canal and feels like one downtown neighbourhood with Centretown, but it is Glebe C.I.’s. The third is the western option area — Wellington West, Hintonburg and Bayview are a board-published choice area, covered in the gifted-draw section below, not home catchment.

Lisgar Collegiate Institute — The Facts

Lisgar Collegiate Institute is Ottawa’s oldest secondary school — founded in 1843, in a heritage-designated building at 29 Lisgar Street, between Elgin Street and the Rideau Canal. About 900 students attend, in Grades 9 to 12. The school’s own summary of its catchment runs “from LeBreton Flats, Centretown, Sandy Hill to Rockcliffe Park” — the board’s boundary adds New Edinburgh, Lindenlea and part of Old Ottawa East to that line.

The program shelf is why families cross the city for this school: Advanced Placement including the Capstone diploma, Extended French and French Immersion certificates with DELF testing, and two Specialist High Skills Majors — Hospitality and Applied Research — alongside the congregated gifted program covered below. One correction worth making plainly, because third-party sites get it wrong: Lisgar does not offer an IB program.

Two Doors Into Lisgar — The Catchment and the Gifted Draw

The first door is the one this guide maps: the home catchment. An address inside the boundary carries the regular English program and the French Immersion program at Lisgar for Grades 9 to 12 — that is what the listings above are filtered on, and it is the only door an address opens.

The second door is separate. Lisgar has hosted the board’s congregated gifted classes since 1957 — the first program of its kind in Ontario — and it is one of three OCDSB secondary gifted sites. Gifted placement is a board-wide draw run by the board: it is not part of the home catchment, living inside this boundary is neither required nor a guarantee, and no address in this guide should be read as a gifted placement. Families weighing the gifted program should confirm the current process with the board directly.

One more board layer sits just west of the line. For Wellington West, Hintonburg and Bayview, the board publishes an option area — its own sheet names a choice of Glebe C.I., Lisgar C.I. or Nepean H.S. Those homes are not in the Lisgar home catchment, they are not in this guide’s searches, and the choice is the board’s to confirm for an exact address. If you are buying in the option area for a Lisgar placement, get the board’s answer in writing before you firm up.

The Fraser Rating — One Measure, In Context

The Fraser Institute’s 2025 Report Card on Ontario’s Secondary Schools rates Lisgar Collegiate Institute 8.1 out of 10, ranked #79 of 747 Ontario secondary schools, on 2023/24 data. On the Institute’s five-year view the school rates 8.3 and ranks #34 — one of the stronger five-year records in the city.

Hold the number the way it deserves to be held. The rating is built mostly on provincial test results — EQAO and OSSLT outcomes and the gaps between them — and Lisgar’s intake includes a board-wide gifted draw, which shapes its results in ways a neighbourhood school’s intake does not. It says nothing about the building, the teachers your child actually gets, or the programs above. Use it as one input. The families who choose Lisgar usually choose it for the programs and the downtown campus — the rating follows the school, not the other way around.

Lisgar or Glebe? The Downtown Line

Downtown Ottawa has two OCDSB secondaries, and the line between them is the question this guide gets most. Lisgar takes the core north of the canal and the Queensway corridor — LeBreton Flats, Centretown, Sandy Hill and the river neighbourhoods. The Glebe, south of the canal, is Glebe Collegiate Institute’s catchment — close enough to walk between the two schools, and a different assignment all the same.

The practical rule is the same one that runs this whole series: the address decides, and on the boundary streets — the canal-side blocks, the Old Ottawa East reach — it decides house by house. Questions Families Ask About the Lisgar Catchment

Which neighbourhoods are inside the Lisgar Collegiate Institute catchment?
On the Ottawa-Carleton District School Board’s current published boundary: LeBreton Flats, Centretown and the downtown core, Sandy Hill, New Edinburgh, Lindenlea and Rockcliffe Park, and part of Old Ottawa East — 9,022 civic addresses on 278 streets. The Glebe is Glebe Collegiate’s catchment, and Vanier and Overbrook, east of the Rideau River, are outside it.

Is the Glebe inside the Lisgar catchment?
No. The Glebe is Glebe C.I.’s catchment. Lisgar’s southern edge runs along the Rideau Canal and the Queensway corridor; homes south of that line path to Glebe, however downtown they feel. The two schools are a short walk apart — the assignment is not interchangeable.

Does living in the catchment get my child into Lisgar’s gifted program?
No. Lisgar’s congregated gifted classes — running since 1957, the first in Ontario, and one of three OCDSB secondary gifted sites — are a separate board-wide draw run by the board. The catchment governs the regular English and French Immersion programs. A Lisgar-catchment address is neither required for gifted placement nor a guarantee of it.

What programs does Lisgar offer?
Advanced Placement, including the AP Capstone diploma; Extended French and French Immersion certificates with DELF testing; and two Specialist High Skills Majors — Hospitality, and Applied Research — alongside the congregated gifted program. Lisgar does not offer an IB program, whatever third-party sites claim.

What about the option area in Wellington West, Hintonburg and Bayview?
The board publishes an option sheet for that area naming a choice of Glebe C.I., Lisgar C.I. or Nepean H.S. It is a choice area, not part of the Lisgar home catchment — the listings in this guide do not include it, and the board confirms placement for an exact address. If a Lisgar placement is the reason for buying there, get the board’s answer before you firm up.

How does Lisgar Collegiate rate academically?
The Fraser Institute’s 2025 Report Card rates Lisgar 8.1 out of 10, ranked #79 of 747 Ontario secondary schools on 2023/24 data; on the five-year view it rates 8.3 and ranks #34. It is one measure, built mostly on provincial test results — and Lisgar’s intake includes a board-wide gifted draw, which shapes those results. Treat it as a starting point, not a verdict.

How do I confirm a specific address is in the Lisgar catchment?
Use the OCDSB school locator, or call the school office — and do it before you offer, especially on the split streets: Beechwood Avenue, Echo Drive and the canal-side blocks are decided house by house. Or send me the address and I will confirm it the same day.
