For the Partner at the Kitchen Table What a Brokerage Change Actually Means for Your Household

The short version: Your partner is thinking of changing the company they work through. Their clients stay their clients. Their licence stays their licence. Homes still get sold the same way. What changes is behind the scenes: what the company keeps from each sale, what help your partner gets finding new clients, and who they can call when they are stuck. This page explains it in plain words, so you can talk about it together at home.

First, the Words — in Plain Language

TL;DR: Four words explain almost everything. A brokerage is the company an agent must legally work through. The split is how each commission is divided. The cap is a yearly limit on the company's share. A lead is a person who has asked about buying or selling.

  • Brokerage. The company your partner legally works through. Agents in Ontario cannot sell homes fully on their own — they must belong to one. Changing brokerages means changing that company.
  • Commission. The fee paid when a home sells, usually a percentage of the price. It is only paid when a sale closes. No sale, no commission — the same everywhere.
  • Split. How the commission is divided. An 80/20 split means your partner keeps 80 cents of each commission dollar; the company keeps 20.
  • Cap. A limit. Once the company has received a set amount in one year, it stops taking its share, apart from small set fees on each sale.
  • Lead. A person who has shown interest in buying or selling. A lead is not a client yet, and it is not income. It is a chance to earn a client.

What Actually Changes for Your Household

TL;DR: Three things change. What the company keeps goes down once the cap is reached. A free program that helps find new clients gets set up. And your partner joins a weekly group call with other agents for ideas and support.

The money the company keeps. At eXp Realty in Canada, your partner keeps 80 percent of each commission until the company has received $16,000 in a year — reached at $80,000 of commission earned. After that, your partner keeps the whole commission, minus a set fee on each sale: $285 a sale for the next 20 sales, then $75 a sale. There is also a monthly fee of $139, a one-time starting fee of $199, and a $29 fee on each sale for the company's review of the paperwork. The $16,000 limit starts again each year, on the anniversary of joining. In household words: the company's slice has a ceiling, and after the ceiling, almost all of each cheque stays with your partner.

Help finding clients, at no cost. When an agent joins eXp through Tomasz R. Witek and my senior partner, as co-sponsors, a free lead generation program is set up for them. In plain words: a team whose job is finding people who may want to buy or sell sets up a system that brings those enquiries to your partner, at no cost. The people who enquire are your partner's contacts to keep. It does not promise sales — it promises a steady flow of real enquiries, instead of paying for adverts or waiting for the phone to ring.

People to learn from, every week. Your partner also joins a weekly mastermind — simply a group call where experienced agents share what is working. No one sells anything on the call. It is the staff-room your partner may not have now, as many agents work alone from home.

What Does Not Change

TL;DR: Your partner's clients, licence, and daily work stay the same. Pay still arrives when a home closes, as now. Nothing is asked of you.

  • The clients stay. Families your partner has helped keep your partner's name and number. A company change does not hand clients to anyone else.
  • The licence stays. Your partner is licensed in Ontario, and that licence simply moves to the new company. No new exam, no starting over.
  • The work stays. Showing homes, advising sellers, negotiating offers — the daily job is identical.
  • Payday works the same way. Money still arrives when a sale closes, through the brokerage, as it does now. There is no salary before and no salary after.
  • Nothing is asked of you. You do not sign anything and you do not register for anything. This page exists only so the decision is not a mystery to you.

Will our income drop while my partner makes the change?

There is no planned gap. Sales in progress are handled under rules agreed when the move is made, and new sales are paid through the new company as they close. Honestly, income follows closings, and closings do not always land evenly — but the change itself does not stop pay arriving, and no one can promise a smooth month in a commission job, at any company.

Is there a catch with the free client-finding program?

It is free because it is arranged as part of joining through Tom and my senior partner. What it is not is a promise. It brings enquiries; your partner still has to call them, earn their trust, and do the work. Think of it as a tap being turned on, not a cheque being written.

Questions Worth Asking Together

TL;DR: Sit down with last year's numbers. Add up what the current company actually kept. Then compare it, line by line, with the ceiling described above.

You do not need to love real estate to judge this well. You just need last year's real numbers:

  • How much commission did your partner earn last year, in total?
  • How much did the current company keep — the split, plus every monthly fee and per-sale charge?
  • Is there a ceiling on what the current company can keep in a year? If not, it earns more every time your partner earns more, forever.
  • What help does the current company give your partner to find the next client — and what does that help cost?

Those answers settle most of the discussion, with a calculator instead of opinions. One more thing, plainly: agents at eXp can also receive a share of company revenue when agents they introduced are working, and shares in the parent company for strong results. It is extra, it varies, and it should never be the reason for a move. Amounts vary and are not guaranteed — see eXp's income disclosure.
