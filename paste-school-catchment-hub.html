The Short Answer

Ottawa school catchments are set by each school board, program track by program track — English, Early French Immersion, and special programs can each carry a different line for the same school. The OCDSB publishes its official boundary maps on its secondary boundary index and elementary boundary index, and its School Locator — listed as up to date for the 2026–2027 school year — answers for one exact address at a time. This series takes six high-demand OCDSB catchments — three in Kanata, one in Beacon Hill / Gloucester, and two in Barrhaven — and builds each one a full homes-for-sale guide from its official line.

How a Catchment Search Actually Works

A catchment (the board calls it an attendance boundary) is the area a school draws its students from. Four things about it matter to a buyer:

  • The board decides, not the map. A line that looks obvious — the school at the end of the street — is not evidence. The board’s locator result on the exact address is.

  • Every track has its own line. English and Immersion entries are published separately on the OCDSB index, and a program like Colonel By’s International Baccalaureate carries a third, separate boundary.

  • Every board has its own lines. The Catholic and French-language boards draw from the same streets differently. An OCDSB answer says nothing about the school across the road run by another board.

  • Lines move. Growth, new schools and enrolment pressure all trigger reviews — which is why this series re-checks its catchments every September.

Catchment boundaries in this series are drawn from the OCDSB’s official boundary indexes, retrieved November 2, 2026. School assignment is the board’s decision — verify your exact address with the board’s school locator before you firm up an offer.

How Each Spoke Is Verified Before It Publishes

Every guide in this series passes the same five steps, in order, before a single figure appears on it:

  • Boundary source saved. The school’s official OCDSB boundary entry, with its retrieval date, filed with the spoke.

  • Listings pulled inside the line. Active and sold listings for the area inside the official boundary — not for the neighbourhood that shares the school’s name.

  • The search built from the line. The live-search area is derived from the official boundary, with the standard filters, and saved verbatim — never redrawn from memory.

  • Three addresses checked. One deep inside, one at the edge, one just outside — run through the board’s school locator. The spoke publishes only if the search area and the locator agree on all three.

  • Sold figures on a 360-day window. Averages and medians from a maximum of the last 360 days of sales, at least three listings behind every figure, never an individual sold price.

Questions Families Ask First

 

Does buying a home inside a catchment guarantee my child a place at that school?

 

No. School assignment is always the school board’s decision, boundaries can change, and programs fill. A catchment line is a strong starting point for a search — never a guarantee. Confirm the schools assigned to the exact address with the board’s school locator, and directly with any other school you are considering, before you firm up an offer.

 

How do I check which school an Ottawa address belongs to?

 

Use the Ottawa-Carleton District School Board’s official School Locator, which the board lists as up to date for the 2026–2027 school year. Enter the exact address — not just the street or the neighbourhood — because a boundary can run along a street and put one side in a different catchment from the other.

 

Are the English and French Immersion boundaries for the same school the same line?

 

Not necessarily. The OCDSB publishes separate boundary entries by program track, and they do not have to match. Colonel By Secondary School is the clearest example in this pilot: its International Baccalaureate program has its own attendance boundary, separate from its English and Immersion boundary. Each spoke in this series states the track it covers, from the board’s own index.

 

What about Catholic or French-language schools at the same address?

 

Different boards draw different lines. The Ottawa Catholic School Board and the French-language boards set their own catchments, and the same address can fall in a different zone for each board. This pilot series covers OCDSB catchments; Catholic-board mirrors of the pilot zones are planned as a later phase. Whatever board your family is considering, verify the exact address with that board.

 

When will each catchment guide in this series be published?

 

One at a time, and only after its data workflow is complete: the official boundary saved, the listings pulled for the area inside the line, the search built from that line, sample addresses checked against the board’s locator, and the sold figures calculated on a maximum 360-day window. This hub links all six guides from day one — each link opens its guide the day that guide publishes.

 

Do school catchment boundaries change?

 

Yes. Boards review boundaries as neighbourhoods grow and schools open, fill, or close. That is why every page in this series names the board source it was drawn from, why the Shortlist carries a September re-check date, and why the verification step — the board’s locator, on the exact address — belongs in every purchase, every time.

 

Why does a home on my street belong to a different catchment than the school at the end of it?

 

Because catchment lines are drawn by the board to balance enrolment across schools, not to follow what looks natural on a map. A line can split a neighbourhood, run down the middle of a street, or send one pocket of homes to a school that is not the closest building. Proximity is a hint; the locator result on the exact address is the answer.
