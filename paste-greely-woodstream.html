The community — six streets around a lake

Woodstream is simple to describe: six streets, 155 homes in the City of Ottawa’s address records, around the community’s own lake in Greely’s north end. Suncrest Drive is the marquee street — the lake living the community is sold on — with Woodstream Drive and Woods Lake Road beside it, and Tintern Drive, Rousseau Crescent and Wild Fern Way completing the set. City building-permit records put Woodstream homes on Plan 4M-1305. Sunset Lakes Developments repeated here the formula that had worked at Sunset Lakes: estate lots around a spring-fed lake, with the owners’ association running the amenity set — a sandy beach, a pool and tennis. There are no new lots releasing: Woodstream is a finished, established resale community, where what comes to market is a known quantity.

The character is in the setting: mature streets and trees, homes on the private well-and-septic estate format, and the lake at the community’s heart — lots backing onto the water are the prized ones. It sits at the village’s north end, about a kilometre north of Stanley Park and just west of the village core, close enough for daily life and quiet in the way the north end of Greely is quiet.

Living in Woodstream

Daily life here is the lake-community end of Greely: six quiet streets, mature trees, the beach and the association amenities in season, and no through traffic. The village core is about 2.8 km east — The Shoppes of Greely plaza on Bank Street for groceries and everyday services, the Greely Community Centre and the library branch on Meadow Drive. Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31) is the spine north: downtown Ottawa is roughly half an hour in good traffic. The honest transit picture matches the rest of Greely: no regular OC Transpo route runs through the village — the Thursday Shopper Route 304 is the one exception — so Woodstream is a driver’s address, and school buses, run by the Ottawa Student Transportation Authority, are the transit that matters to families here.

What does the owners’ association run?
The amenity set the community is sold on: the sandy beach on the lake, the pool and tennis — the family version of the Sunset Lakes lake life, run through the Woodstream owners’ association. The homes themselves sit on the private well-and-septic estate format, on mature streets with the trees up.

Same developer and same formula — Sunset Lakes Developments, estate lots around a spring-fed lake, an owners’ association and design review — in a different corner of the village: Sunset Lakes is the original, in Greely’s northwest; Woodstream repeated the formula at the village’s north end.
