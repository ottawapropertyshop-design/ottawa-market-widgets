The kinds of land that come up in Greely

Greely land is not one product. Four kinds of parcels come up in the Greely market, and they price — and sell — very differently:

  • Registered-plan estate lots. Single lots inside finished estate plans — the Emerald Links Phase 3 lots on Green Links Way, the remaining Cedarlakes-phase lots, infill lots in the village plans. The plan is registered, the lot dimensions are on the plan, and what you can build is set by the zoning and the plan's own controls.

  • Rural acreage on the through-roads. Parcels on Stagecoach Road, Parkway Road, River Road, Old Prescott Road and Mitch Owens Road — typically a few acres, usually unserviced, where the buyer handles the well, the septic, the entrance and the build.

  • Farm and working land. Larger parcels in active or past farm use — the Shields Creek flats, the aggregate lands around the Osgoode Sand & Gravel operation on Old Prescott Road, and the big rural lots south and east of the village. Zoning, soil and frontage drive the value here.

  • Village and future-phase land. Parcels in and around the Village Core and the edges of the built-up communities — the land the next Greely phases will be built on, plus the odd infill lot inside the village itself.

Buying land in Greely — what to check before you offer

A Greely land purchase rewards homework more than a house purchase does, because the parcel in front of you is only half the story. The checks that matter most:

  • Zoning and permitted use. Confirm the zoning and what it allows — a house, a farm use, a home business — before you fall for the acreage. Rural and agricultural zones in Greely carry real restrictions.

  • Well and septic. Most rural Greely land is unserviced: budget for a drilled well and a septic system, and check what the soil and the lot size allow. Village-core parcels can differ — the servicing answer is per-parcel, never assumed.

  • Water setbacks and floodplain. Parcels near Shields Creek, the Osgoode Garden Creek and Cedar Lake can carry conservation-authority setbacks that shrink the buildable area. Check before you design the house around the view.

  • Severances. Splitting a lot off a larger parcel goes through the City of Ottawa consent process, and frontage, zoning minimums and servicing decide whether it is possible at all. Never price a parcel on a severance that has not been confirmed.

  • HST and closing costs. Vacant land sold by a builder, a developer or a business usually carries HST; a private sale of personal-use land can be treated differently. Confirm the tax treatment with your lawyer or accountant before you offer, not after.

Greely land — questions buyers ask

Is Greely land serviced?
Most rural Greely land is private well and septic — that is the default assumption. Lots inside the village core and some finished plans can differ, so the answer is settled per parcel, from the listing and the City's servicing records, before you offer.

Can I buy a big parcel and sever a lot off it?
Sometimes — severances in Greely go through the City of Ottawa consent process, and the tests are frontage, minimum lot size under the zoning, and servicing. A parcel that cannot be severed is worth materially less than the same acres that can, so confirm before you price the idea into an offer.

Is HST charged on vacant land in Greely?
Usually, when the seller is a builder, a developer or a business. A private individual selling personal-use land can fall outside that. It is one of the most common surprises in land deals — get the answer in writing from your lawyer or accountant early.

How big are Greely estate lots?
Large, by Ottawa standards — the estate plans around Greely (Emerald Links, Cedarlakes, Greely Orchard) run around an acre and up per lot, and the rural parcels run from a couple of acres to full farm lots. The registered plan or the survey states the exact size; never rely on a listing's rounding.

Why do two Greely parcels of the same size sell for such different prices?
Because land value in Greely sits in the servicing, the zoning, the road frontage, the buildable area after setbacks, and how close the parcel is to the village core — not in the raw acreage. Two ten-acre parcels can be different products entirely.
