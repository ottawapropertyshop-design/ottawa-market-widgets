The Manotick East story — how this Manotick estate country came to be

Manotick East is the east-bank half of an old township landscape. The ground is the former Township of Osgoode — surveyed in concessions and lots, opened in 1798, with the first patents on its river frontage granted as early as 1801 and its first settler, Archibald Macdonell, arriving in 1826. The estate pockets of today’s Manotick East sit on Part of Lots 2, 3 and 4, Concession 2 of that survey — farm lots that fronted the Rideau River and the roads running south from Manotick village. Two institutions anchored the east bank early: a hamlet and a railway.

Herberts Corners — the crossroads where Herberts Corners Road meets the Manotick Station Road corridor — takes its name from the Herbert family, Irish settlers of the school section. A log school first opened at the corners in November 1844; Patrick Herbert donated school land in 1876, and in 1890 Michael Herbert and his wife Ellen Nash sold the school site to the trustees for $35, on a deed signed by James Herbert. The hamlet never became a village — it stayed a crossroads of farms — but its name still labels the south end of Manotick East, and Herberts Corners Road still carries estate homes through it.

Manotick Station exists because of the Bytown and Prescott Railway, the line that tied Ottawa to the St. Lawrence. The railway reached the future station ground on November 4, 1854, and the first train ran on Christmas Day 1854; a new station building was going up by February 1871. The line became the St. Lawrence and Ottawa Railway and was leased to the Canadian Pacific in 1884 as its Prescott Subdivision. The last scheduled passenger trains ran on October 26, 1957, freight service was discontinued in 1998 and the rails came up in May 1999 — but the station building survives, moved to 6020 Mitch Owens Road as a private home, one of only two St. Lawrence and Ottawa stations left standing. Manotick Station Road still carries the name, and the corridor along it is Manotick East’s spine.


Lancer Avenue, at the newest Rideau Forest lands in Manotick East — the enclave is still being built out, one phase at a time.

Rideau Forest is the development that made Manotick East an estate address. The vendor of record, Rideau Forest Development Ltd., works from 5542 Ann Street in Manotick village — the same address as Ken Gordon Holdings, the Gordon family’s development company. The model was land, not houses: serviced estate lots released in phases, each buyer building a custom home with their own builder and architect, working drawings approved in writing by the developer before a building permit, and clearing limited to the approved building envelope so the forest survives every build. The oldest part of the enclave is Phase 1, around Scobie Crescent, where the community’s park and courts sit. The northern phases are the documented modern record: Phases 7 to 11 were filed as one draft plan (City file D07-16-03-0036) before July 2003 and draft-approved on August 25, 2006. Phase 7 alone is 40 lots on 46.69 hectares of the old Concession 2 farm lots; Phase 8 is Registered Plan 4M-1406; and a 13-hectare nature reserve under Registered Plan 4M-1346 is co-owned by the lot owners of the northern phases. Phase 9 — part of 1120 Manotick Station Road, about 34 hectares and 43 lots — was still moving through its registration conditions in 2021. The phases can proceed at all only because the draft approvals predate the current Official Plan: rural estate lot subdivisions are no longer permitted in Ottawa, and grandfathering is the whole reason Manotick East is still growing.

Adams Estates repeated the pattern one concession east. The plan of subdivision for 6202 Nick Adams Road (City file D07-16-12-0006) was filed on November 14, 2012, and Council approved its rezoning on May 10, 2017 (By-law 2017-146) — two-acre-plus estate lots on a cul-de-sac off Manotick Station Road, marketed with custom builders including Omega Homes and since sold out. Between the phases, the pockets and the one-off rural builds, that is the whole Manotick East method: big lots, custom homes, no production builders, and a planning record that cannot be started again.

Manotick East questions, answered

Where is Manotick East?
Manotick East is OREB district 8005 — the east bank of the Rideau River at Manotick, inside the City of Ottawa: from Mitch Owens Road in the north, between River Road and Stagecoach Road, south past Manotick Station and Herberts Corners into the farm concessions. It holds 1,097 homes on 50 streets in the City’s address records.

Who subdivided Manotick East’s estate pockets?
Rideau Forest was subdivided by Rideau Forest Development Ltd. of 5542 Ann Street, Manotick — the Gordon family’s development operation, which sold serviced lots in phases under design control instead of building houses. Its northern phases (7 to 11) were draft-approved in 2006 under City file D07-16-03-0036, and Phase 8 is Registered Plan 4M-1406. Adams Estates, off Nick Adams Road, was filed in 2012 and rezoned in 2017. The rest of Manotick East is older township farm fabric — concessions, crossroads and one-off rural builds.

How big are the lots in Manotick East?
Estate scale. Rideau Forest’s later phases and Adams Estates are built on the two-acre class, and the district’s rural homes sit on farm-sized parcels along the concessions. Even the village-edge streets carry lots far beyond suburban dimensions — space is the district’s defining feature.

What services do Manotick East homes run on?
The estate pockets have underground hydro, cable, internet and natural gas at the lot; every home runs on its own private well and septic system. Budget the homework into any purchase: water potability and flow tests, a septic inspection, and conservation-authority review where a regulated feature touches the lot.

Which schools serve Manotick East?
For the Rideau Forest pocket and the streets around it: Manotick Public School (JK–5) → Kars on the Rideau (6–8) → Osgoode Township High School and South Carleton High School, both listed by the board’s locator; St. Mary (JK–6) → St. Mark Catholic High School (7–12), which sits inside the district on Dozois Road; Michel-Dupuis → Pierre-de-Blois in French public; and Jonathan-Pitre → Pierre-Savard by exact-address designation in French Catholic. Earlscourt Crescent and Squire Drive go to St. Leonard instead of St. Mary, and the Caballero Avenue streets follow Greely Elementary → Metcalfe Public. Confirm any exact address in the board’s locator before you buy.

Can I still buy a lot and build in Manotick East?
Yes — while lots last. Rideau Forest’s remaining phases and resale estate lots reach the market one at a time, and the live land panel in this guide tracks the district’s shelf. New rural estate subdivisions can no longer be started under the current Official Plan, so the existing phases and parcels are the whole supply.
