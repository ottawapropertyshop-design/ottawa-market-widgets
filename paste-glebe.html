The Short Version

Glebe Collegiate Institute is the designated public secondary school (OCDSB, Grades 9–12) for the Glebe, Old Ottawa South, Old Ottawa East and the Dow’s Lake and canal-side streets. The school stands at 212 Glebe Avenue, in the middle of the neighbourhood it is named for. This guide maps the board’s own attendance boundary exactly as published, and every listings section below is drawn from inside that line — 8,003 civic addresses on 196 streets.

Two things catch downtown buyers out. First, the line is not the downtown core: Centretown, the downtown towers and Sandy Hill, north of the canal and the Queensway, belong to Lisgar Collegiate Institute — Glebe’s sister school and its mirror on the north side of the line. Second, Glebe is also the board’s designated French Immersion gifted site, which is a separate board-wide draw that has nothing to do with the neighbourhood line. Both doors are explained below.

The Catchment, Street by Street

The core is the Glebe itself: the numbered avenues — First, Second, Third, Fourth and Fifth — running between Bronson Avenue and the Rideau Canal, with Clemow Avenue’s wide estate lots, Glebe Avenue past the school’s own front door, and Bank Street as the commercial spine. South of Bank Street Bridge, Old Ottawa South joins the catchment whole: Sunnyside Avenue, Hopewell Avenue and the streets down to the Rideau River. Across the canal, Old Ottawa East — Echo Drive, Main Street’s north reach and the streets around Immaculata — is inside the line as well, and the western reach takes in the Dow’s Lake shore and a fringe of the Civic Hospital and Little Italy streets.

The edges are decided house by house on the split streets. Bronson Avenue, Preston Street, Bank Street and Echo Drive all cross the line, so one side of a street can be Glebe while the other is not — and Main Street and Riverdale Avenue are only partly inside, at their north ends. Glebe Collegiate Institute — The Facts

Glebe Collegiate Institute is the Ottawa-Carleton District School Board’s largest secondary school, at about 1,500 students, and one of its oldest: it and Lisgar were a single school until the two split in the 1920s, and the pair still anchor downtown secondary education from opposite sides of the canal. Glebe runs the English program, French Immersion and Extended French, an ESL and Special Education Learning Centre, and a Developmental Disabilities Program, with third languages in German, Spanish and Latin.

Its specialist programs are the draw beyond the neighbourhood: two Specialist High Skills Majors — Non-Profit (Social Justice) and Sports — and the International Certificate Program. And layered over everything is the gifted designation: Glebe is the board’s designated French Immersion gifted site, so its congregated gifted classrooms draw immersion students from across the central and east board, by application, not by address.

Two Doors Into Glebe — the Catchment and the Gifted Draw

There are two distinct ways a student ends up at Glebe, and confusing them is the most common mistake I see downtown. The first door is the catchment itself: live inside the boundary in this guide and Glebe C.I. is your designated secondary school for its English program, French Immersion and Extended French. The second door is the board’s specialized gifted program: Glebe is the OCDSB’s designated French Immersion gifted site, and its congregated gifted classes — in English, Mathematics, Social Sciences and Science, Grades 9–12 — are filled from a board-wide draw of identified gifted immersion students. That draw reaches far beyond this map.

Between the two doors sit the board’s published option areas — addresses that may elect Glebe or Hillcrest, Glebe or Lisgar or Nepean, or Glebe or Nepean. An option is a choice the board offers, not a designation your address carries, and it can change when the board revises its sheets. If you are buying in an option area, price the home on the home, and treat the school choice as a question to confirm with the board in writing — never as a feature the seller can promise you.

The Fraser Rating — In Context

The Fraser Institute’s 2025 Report Card on Ontario’s secondary schools (built on 2023/24 data) rates Glebe C.I. 7.5 out of 10, ranked 142 of 747 schools province-wide, with a five-year rating of 7.6 and a five-year rank of 101. The honest read: a mid-pack provincial band on the Institute’s test-based measures, at a school whose waiting demand has never been about that band. I publish the number because buyers ask for it, with its band attached — no inflation, no cherry-picking.

What the rating does not measure is the reason families move here: the immersion gifted draw, the two SHSM programs, the athletics, the canal-side neighbourhood and a student body drawn from some of the city’s most engaged households. Use the Fraser figure as one input. The fuller picture is the program list above, a school visit, and the board’s own reporting — in that order.

Glebe or Lisgar? The Downtown Line

Because the two schools began as one, downtown buyers constantly weigh them against each other. The line between them runs along the canal and the Queensway corridor: south of it, the Glebe, Old Ottawa South and Old Ottawa East are Glebe’s; north of it, Centretown, the downtown core, Sandy Hill, Lowertown, New Edinburgh and Rockcliffe Park are Lisgar’s. The programs mirror each other — Lisgar is the board’s congregated English gifted site, Glebe its French Immersion gifted site — so for gifted families the language of instruction, not the map, usually decides.

For everyone else, the deciding fact is the exact address. Streets near the line — Echo Drive, the canal-side blocks, the Queensway edge — are where a guess costs the most. Questions Families Ask

Which neighbourhoods are inside the Glebe Collegiate Institute catchment?
The Glebe itself, Old Ottawa South, Old Ottawa East, and the Dow’s Lake and canal-side streets, plus a western reach into the Civic Hospital and Little Italy fringe — 8,003 civic addresses on 196 streets in all. The numbered avenues (First to Fifth), Clemow Avenue and the streets around the school are the core. Centretown and the downtown core, north of the canal and the Queensway, are not in this catchment — they belong to Lisgar.

Is Centretown inside the Glebe catchment?
No. Centretown, the downtown towers, Sandy Hill, Lowertown, New Edinburgh and Rockcliffe Park are Lisgar Collegiate Institute’s catchment. The two schools were one school until the 1920s split, and the canal-and-Queensway corridor is still roughly the line between them. If a downtown address matters to you, check which side of that line it sits on — the guides for both schools are in the series table at the bottom of this page.

Does living in the catchment get my child into Glebe’s gifted program?
No — the two are separate. Glebe is the OCDSB’s designated French Immersion gifted site, and its congregated gifted classes are filled by a board-wide application and draw, not by address. Living inside the catchment designates Glebe as your child’s home school for the English program, French Immersion and Extended French; the gifted door is a second door, explained in its own section above.

What programs does Glebe Collegiate Institute offer?
The English program, French Immersion and Extended French, an ESL and Special Education Learning Centre, and a Developmental Disabilities Program. Its Specialist High Skills Majors are Non-Profit (Social Justice) and Sports, and it runs the board’s International Certificate Program. Third languages are German, Spanish and Latin. It is the board’s largest secondary school, at about 1,500 students.

What about the board’s option areas for Glebe?
The board publishes option (choice) areas around Glebe: addresses that may elect Glebe or Hillcrest, Glebe or Lisgar or Nepean, or Glebe or Nepean. Those are choice zones, not home catchment — they are not counted in this guide’s 8,003 addresses and the listings here do not include them. If you are buying in one of those zones, the choice itself is part of the decision, so confirm the current option sheet for the exact address before you buy.

How does Glebe rate academically?
The Fraser Institute’s 2025 Report Card (2023/24 data) rates Glebe 7.5 out of 10, ranked 142 of 747 Ontario secondary schools, with a five-year rating of 7.6 and a five-year rank of 101. That is a mid-pack provincial band on test-based measures, and it is worth reading honestly: Glebe’s demand has never rested on that number alone — it rests on the gifted and immersion programs, the athletics, and the neighbourhood. Treat the rating as one measure among several, never as a promise about an individual child.
