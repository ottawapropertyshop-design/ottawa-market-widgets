The Catchment, Street by Street

The school itself stands on Charlemagne Boulevard in Fallingbrook, in the catchment’s northwest — and Fallingbrook is inside whole: the Princess Louise Drive and Gardenway streets, Watters Road, and the Tenth Line Road corridor down to Innes Road. West of Tenth Line, the boundary steps out to take in the Tompkins Avenue and Prestwick Drive block — Queenswood Heights paths to St. Peter on the board’s map even though it sits on the far side of the road — while Sunview Drive, Notre-Dame Street, Champlain Street and Vineyard Drive, just to the south, stay outside it. That one step west is the most misread line in east Orléans.

The neighbourhoods of the St. Peter catchment on one map — every pin sits in its true direction from St. Peter Catholic High School, and the legend gives each area’s true straight-line distance from the school.

South of Fallingbrook, the boundary takes in Notting Gate and Notting Hill, the Esprit Drive streets and the Mer Bleue Road corridor — the Avalon side of Orléans paths to St. Peter, down both sides of Mer Bleue as far as the rural edge. East along the river, the shore is inside in whole: Trim Road and Petrie Island, Tweddle Road, the Cumberland village streets and the riverfront lots out to Canaan Road, the city’s eastern edge.

South of the suburb the catchment turns rural and stays big. Navan and its concession roads are inside, Sarsfield is inside, and the line runs down the Frank Kenny and Dunning Road corridors through Carlsbad Springs to the Russland Road boundary in the south. The long roads split along the way — Innes Road, St-Joseph Boulevard, Navan Road, Russell Road and Tenth Line Road each carry a St. Peter stretch and a stretch that belongs to another school — so on those roads the civic number, not the street name, decides the school.

The edges matter as much as the middle. West of the line — Convent Glen, Orléans Woods, Chapel Hill and Châteauneuf — is St. Matthew’s, and Blackburn Hamlet moved to St. Matthew in September 2025. East of Canaan Road is Clarence-Rockland, outside Ottawa entirely. South of Russland Road the Russell Road side paths to St. Francis Xavier. On any street near those edges, the exact address decides — which is what the free guide below is for.

St. Peter Catholic High School — The Facts

St. Peter Catholic High School, at 750 Charlemagne Boulevard — (613) 837-9377 — is the Ottawa Catholic School Board’s English Catholic secondary school for the eastern half of Orléans, Cumberland and the rural east. It teaches Grades 7 to 12 in one building, so a child who arrives in Grade 7 stays through graduation. The school — home of the Knights — opened in September 1992, taking over the eastern half of the suburb from St. Matthew, which had carried all of Orléans’ English Catholic students alone since 1981. A major wing followed in 2005 as Fallingbrook and the southern suburbs filled in around it.

The School Pathway Into St. Peter

The path, in one line: OCSB elementary school, Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6 → St. Peter Catholic High School, Grades 7 to 12.

Five Ottawa Catholic School Board elementary schools feed St. Peter, named on the board’s own boundary map for the school. Each teaches Junior Kindergarten to Grade 6:

St. Francis of Assisi School — 795 Watters Road. The feeder for Fallingbrook, the neighbourhood wrapped around the high school itself — its graduates walk five minutes up Charlemagne Boulevard.

St. Clare School — 2133 Gardenway Drive. The feeder for the Gardenway streets and the eastern side of Fallingbrook.

St. Theresa School — 2000 Portobello Boulevard. The feeder for the Portobello and Mer Bleue corridor streets south of Innes Road.

Our Lady of Wisdom School — 1565 St. Georges Street. The feeder for Queenswood Heights and the Tompkins Avenue block west of Tenth Line Road.

St. Dominic School — 2300 Esprit Drive. The feeder for Notting Gate and the Esprit Drive streets at the suburb’s southern edge.

Which feeder serves your child is decided by your exact address, not by which school is closest — and on split streets near the boundary, two houses doors apart can feed different schools. Confirm the address before you buy, every time.

The Fraser Rating — One Measure, In Context

The Fraser Institute’s 2025 Report Card on Ontario’s secondary schools rates St. Peter Catholic High School 6.5 out of 10, ranked #311 of 747 Ontario secondary schools, on 2023/24 data. That places St. Peter just behind St. Matthew (6.9) among Orléans’ two English Catholic secondaries, and mid-pack among Orléans secondaries overall: the French-language schools rate higher (Garneau 7.9, Mer Bleue 7.6, Béatrice-Desloges 7.5), and the English public schools Sir Wilfrid Laurier (6.1) and Cairine Wilson (5.9) rate lower.

Treat the rating as one measure, because that is what it is: it is built largely on provincial test results, and it says nothing about the teachers your child will actually get, the teams and clubs, or the feel of the building on Charlemagne Boulevard. Read it, then walk the school and talk to families whose children go there — that is the research that settles it.

St. Peter or St. Matthew? The Orléans English Catholic Choice

Orléans has two English Catholic secondary schools, and the choice between them is made by your address, not by application. St. Peter takes the east: Fallingbrook, Queenswood Heights, Notting Gate and the Mer Bleue Road corridor, plus Cumberland and the rural east out to Canaan Road and down to Russland Road. St. Matthew takes the west and north of Orléans — Convent Glen, Orléans Woods, Chapel Hill, Châteauneuf — plus Blackburn Hamlet and the west end of the Cumberland shore. Both are Ottawa Catholic School Board schools teaching Grades 7 to 12; on the Fraser Institute’s 2025 Report Card, St. Peter rates 6.5 and St. Matthew 6.9.

The practical point for buyers: the line between the two runs through ordinary suburban streets in Orléans, and on the split streets it is drawn house by house.
