The Short Version

École secondaire publique Louis-Riel is the designated French-language public high school for Ottawa’s east end — Grades 7 to 12 with the Conseil des écoles publiques de l’Est de l’Ontario (CEPEO), the French public board. It stands at 1655 Bearbrook Road in Blackburn Hamlet, it opened in 1980, it teaches about 1,500 students entirely in French, and it is the French public board’s sports school: home of the Sports-études program and the Dôme LR indoor athletics facility. The Fraser Institute rates it 6.6 out of 10, ranked 294th of 747 Ontario secondary schools. Its attendance sector is one of the largest in this series — 170 square kilometres running from Alta Vista and Beacon Hill through Blackburn Hamlet to Convent Glen, Orléans Wood and Chapel Hill, and south to Ramsayville: 41,558 civic addresses on 1,236 streets. This guide draws that official sector on its streets, then shows you only the homes listed inside it — freehold homes, freehold rentals, condominiums, condominium rentals and land, each in its own live search.

The Catchment, Street by Street

Read the sector the way the east end reads itself. At the centre sits Blackburn Hamlet — the garden suburb wrapped in Green’s Creek green space, with the school on Bearbrook Road at its northern edge. North of the hamlet, the line takes in Beacon Hill, Pineview and Carson Grove and the Canotek employment edge. East, it crosses into the western side of Orléans — Convent Glen, Hiawatha Park, Orléans Wood, Chatelaine Village and Chapel Hill — and stops there: Fallingbrook and Avalon, further east, are outside it. The sector’s long western arm runs along Montreal Road past the Montfort Hospital corner and takes in the northern part of Alta Vista and Heron Gate. And its southern reach runs past the Mer Bleue edge through Notre-Dame-des-Champs into the rural lots around Ramsayville.

Three edges are worth reading carefully. Vanier and Overbrook, however close they sit to the western arm, are outside the sector — their French public path runs to a different school. Fallingbrook and Avalon are the eastern trap: Orléans addresses that feel like one community with Chapel Hill belong to the Gisèle-Lalonde sector instead. And the Alta Vista arm is a part-catchment, not the whole neighbourhood — the line crosses Alta Vista, so the exact address, never the neighbourhood name, decides.

École secondaire publique Louis-Riel — The Facts

Louis-Riel opened in the fall of 1980, built for the francophone families of the old Gloucester — the first phase welcomed 250 students, and the students themselves chose the name Louis Riel a year later. A 2003 annex brought Grades 7 and 8 into the building, making it a full Grades 7 to 12 school. Its teams are Les Rebelles, and its students come, in the school’s own words, from every corner of Ottawa’s east end.

One admission fact belongs up front, because it is specific to the French boards: CEPEO schools teach entirely in French and admit students under Ontario’s French-language education rules — children of French-language rights holders, with other cases confirmed by the board. If your family’s eligibility is not straightforward, settle it with the board first; the catchment in this guide decides the school for the students the board admits.

The School Pathway Into Louis-Riel

Louis-Riel is a Grades 7 to 12 school, so the French public path through the east end is short and clean: a CEPEO elementary school for Kindergarten to Grade 6, then straight into Louis-Riel for the full secondary run — no separate middle school in between. On the board’s own published sectors, the elementary sectors for Séraphin-Marion and Marie-Curie sit wholly inside this sector, and L’Odyssée, Le Prélude and L’Aventure overlap it in part. The designated path for an address inside this catchment reads as one clear line:

CEPEO elementary school, Kindergarten to Grade 6 → École secondaire publique Louis-Riel, Grades 7 to 12.

That single line is why the sector matters at purchase time. A family buying inside it is not buying six years of high school — it is buying the whole French public run from Kindergarten to graduation on one board, without a change of school board in between. Confirm the elementary designation for your exact address, because the elementary lines inside the sector are drawn school by school.

One Ottawa rule worth knowing. The CEPEO also publishes an open-choice policy for Ottawa secondary students: to give every student fair access to specialized secondary programs, a student who lives in the Ottawa region may attend the Ottawa CEPEO secondary school of their choice, and the board provides a free OC Transpo PRESTO pass for the trip. The sector on this page remains your designated school — the address guarantee behind this guide — and the choice policy is the board’s own addition on top of it. The school itself settles any individual case.

The Sports School — Sports-études and the Dôme LR

What makes Louis-Riel different from every other school in this series is sport. Its Sports-études program, running since 2005, is built for student-athletes: training happens inside the school day, in athletics, basketball, dance, golf, hockey, alpine skiing, soccer, volleyball, baseball and individual sports, with Specialist High Skills Major academies in basketball, hockey and soccer and high-performance basketball academies playing in provincial and national prep leagues. For a serious young athlete, this program — not the map — is often the reason for the move, and the board’s open-choice policy above is how out-of-sector athletes reach it.

The program’s home is the Dôme LR, the air-supported athletics dome on the school’s own campus on Bearbrook Road: 12,422 square metres holding a 400-metre indoor track — the only one of its kind in North America — built with the Ottawa Lions Track and Field Club. The original dome collapsed in a snowstorm; the board replaced it, and the replacement reached substantial completion in December 2025. A school whose landmark is a national-level training facility tells you something about the community around it: Blackburn Hamlet families fundraised, trained and competed in that dome for twenty years, and they have it back.

The Fraser Rating — One Measure, In Context

The Fraser Institute rated Louis-Riel 6.6 out of 10 in its 2025 Report Card on Ontario’s Secondary Schools — 294th of 747 schools. Read it in its own company: it sits a step above its French public sibling Gisèle-Lalonde (6.5) in the same report card, and it reflects a full French-language program taught to every student in a 170-square-kilometre sector, not a selected intake.

Use the rating the way it deserves to be used: as a measure of past academic results at the school, not a promise about any one child. What the number cannot show is the francophone environment itself, the Sports-études draw, or the walk down Bearbrook Road every morning. Visit the school, walk the streets around it, and let the rating be one input — a fair one — among several.

Louis-Riel or Gisèle-Lalonde? The French Public Choice in the East

Ottawa’s French public map has two secondaries in the east, and Orléans is divided between them. Louis-Riel, from its Blackburn Hamlet campus, takes the west: Blackburn, Beacon Hill, Convent Glen, Orléans Wood and Chapel Hill, plus the Alta Vista arm. Gisèle-Lalonde takes the east of Orléans from boulevard Millennium. One board, two French public high schools, and a sector line running through Orléans between them — if the school is the reason for the move, the address check comes before the offer, every time.

There are English-board mirrors to know as well: the same western Orléans streets sit inside the Cairine Wilson and Sir Wilfrid Laurier catchments for the Ottawa-Carleton board, and the area’s French Catholic secondary is Garneau. Bilingual families often weigh the systems street by street — the guides in this series cover each side of that decision.
