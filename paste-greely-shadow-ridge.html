Shadow Ridge Estates — the community

Shadow Ridge Estates is Greely’s established estate subdivision, sitting between Old Prescott Road and Bank Street, south of Parkway Road — close enough to the village core to use it daily, far enough into the township grid to feel like the country. Its streets — Donwel Drive, Shadow Ridge Drive, Quinnfield Way, Mary Anne Drive, Waterdown Street and Shauna Crescent, with the newer-phase streets of Stonecrop Avenue, Calwood Avenue and Evening Shadow Avenue — carry detached estate homes on fully serviced lots, a different proposition from Greely’s usual well-and-septic acreage.

The brick entrance sign tells the subdivision’s founding story in its own words: this is a community that features ground-source heat pumps and energy-efficient construction — an energy-built subdivision, ahead of its time for a rural Ottawa village. In the middle of it sits the community park — playground, gazebo and split-rail fence — the neighbourhood’s front room.

The communal system — Shadow Ridge’s servicing story

Almost every home in Greely runs on a private well and septic system. Shadow Ridge is the exception — together with Lakeland Meadows, it is the village’s only city-serviced pocket, on communal drinking-water and wastewater systems run by the City of Ottawa. The City treats the pair as one system: its development-charge by-law carries schedules for the “Shadow Ridge and Lakeland Meadows Communal Drinking Water and Wastewater Systems” by name.

The system’s history is the homework. It has operated at or near its rated limits, and its Phase-2 lots were held under a servicing reserve — land that could not proceed until capacity did. Council has approved a Water Plant Upgrade Framework for the system, and the interim “bridge” upgrade is being delivered by the developer at 100% developer cost — the unlock for the held lots and the newer-phase streets. The City monitors the communal wells as a condition of the system’s operation; its reporting tracks a slow upward nitrate trend in the source water, with treated water staying within Ontario’s drinking-water standard.

For a buyer, the servicing cuts both ways, honestly: there is no well to test and no septic to inspect — the two big rural homework items simply do not apply here — but the capacity story is part of the subdivision’s future, and it is worth understanding before you buy. It is also the reason Shadow Ridge reads differently from the rest of Greely: serviced lots, a denser estate fabric, and a village address minutes from the core.

Living in Shadow Ridge

Daily life runs through the park at the subdivision’s heart and the village core about 2 km north — The Shoppes of Greely plaza on Bank Street for groceries and everyday services, the Greely Community Centre and the library branch on Meadow Drive, and Eva’s Chip Wagon up Bank Street when the season opens. Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31) is the spine: downtown Ottawa is roughly half an hour north in good traffic. The honest transit picture: there is no regular OC Transpo route through the village — the one exception is the Thursday Shopper Route 304 — so Shadow Ridge is a driver’s address, and school buses, run by the Ottawa Student Transportation Authority, are the transit that matters to families here.

Shadow Ridge questions, answered

Is Shadow Ridge on city water and sewers?
On communal systems, yes — the City of Ottawa’s communal drinking-water and wastewater systems serve Shadow Ridge and Lakeland Meadows together, the village’s only city-serviced pocket. There is no private well or septic system to test or inspect on a Shadow Ridge purchase.

What kinds of homes are in Shadow Ridge?
Detached estate homes on fully serviced lots. The subdivision was built around energy efficiency — its own entrance sign notes the ground-source heat pumps and energy-efficient construction the community features — and the streets mix the established phases with newer-phase homes on Stonecrop Avenue, Calwood Avenue and Evening Shadow Avenue.

Which schools serve Shadow Ridge?
The designated paths are set out in the schools section above: Greely Elementary School and Castor Valley Elementary School on the public side, Osgoode Township High School for Grades 9 to 12, and St. Mary School to St. Mark Catholic High School on the Catholic side. Confirm any exact address in the board’s school locator before you buy.

How is Shadow Ridge different from Lakeland Meadows?
They are sister subdivisions that share one servicing framework — the City’s development-charge by-law names the “Shadow Ridge and Lakeland Meadows Communal Drinking Water and Wastewater Systems” together. Shadow Ridge is the established community west of the village core; Lakeland Meadows, at 1626 Old Prescott Road, is the newer lot community about 1 km southwest.

How far is Shadow Ridge from downtown Ottawa?
About 25 kilometres — roughly half an hour by car in good traffic, straight up Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31). There is no regular OC Transpo route through the village; the Thursday Shopper Route 304 is the one exception.
