The Catchment, Street by Street

Read the sector the way downtown reads itself. The north end is Lowertown and Sandy Hill — the school’s own streets, the university quarter and the embassy blocks along the Rideau River. The line then takes in New Edinburgh, Lindenlea, Rockcliffe Park and Manor Park along the river’s east bank, runs south through Centretown and the downtown core to the Glebe and Old Ottawa South on the canal, and east across the river into Vanier and Overbrook. Inside the line sit the streets families ask about by name: the Lowertown and Sandy Hill streets a short walk from the school, the Glebe’s canal-side blocks, the Vanier and Overbrook streets across the river, and the Rockcliffe Park and New Edinburgh streets at the sector’s northern reach. That mix is the catchment’s character in one paragraph: heritage streets and condominium towers at the core, and some of the city’s oldest residential streets at its edges.

De La Salle Secondary School — The Facts

De La Salle is downtown Ottawa’s French-language public secondary school — a Lowertown school at 501 Old St Patrick St, Ottawa, ON K1N 8R3 (613-789-0053), teaching entirely in French: for many families that full francophone environment, in the middle of the city, is the point of the move. It is also a magnet school in the truest sense: families choose it from across the city for the two programs it hosts, set out below.

A note on program tracks. De La Salle is home to the Centre d’excellence artistique de l’Ontario — the French-language arts magnet for the province — and to the CEPEO gifted centre. Programs like these are exactly why the board runs an open-choice policy at the secondary level, explained in the pathway section below: living inside the sector designates De La Salle as your teenager’s French public high school; program places are confirmed with the school.

The School Pathway Into De La Salle

De La Salle is a Grades 7 to 12 school, so the French public path through downtown is short and clean: a CEPEO elementary school for Kindergarten to Grade 6, then straight into De La Salle for the full secondary run — no separate middle school in between. The designated path for an address inside this catchment reads as one clear line:

CEPEO elementary school, Kindergarten to Grade 6 → École secondaire publique De La Salle, Grades 7 to 12.

That single line is why the sector matters at purchase time. A family buying inside it is not buying six years of high school — it is buying the whole French public run from Kindergarten to graduation on one board, without a change of school board in between. Confirm the elementary designation for your exact address, because the elementary lines inside the sector are drawn school by school.

The feeders, as the board’s own locator returns them. The secondary designation is De La Salle right across the sector, but the elementary feeders vary by address. For the downtown addresses, the locator designates École élémentaire publique Francojeunesse. In Vanier, it returns two sectors — Mauril-Bélanger and Trille des Bois as option sectors. State your feeder in exactly those terms: there is no single elementary feeder for the whole sector, and the exact address settles which one applies.

One Ottawa rule worth knowing. The CEPEO also publishes an open-choice policy for Ottawa secondary students: to give every student fair access to specialized secondary programs, a student who lives in the Ottawa region may attend the Ottawa CEPEO secondary school of their choice, and the board provides a free OC Transpo PRESTO pass for the trip. The sector on this page remains your designated school — the address guarantee behind this guide — and the choice policy is the board’s own addition on top of it. The school itself, and the transport consortium’s BusPlanner check, settle any individual case.

The Fraser Rating — One Measure, In Context

The Fraser Institute rated De La Salle 6.3 out of 10 in its 2025 Report Card on Ontario secondary schools — 338th of 747 schools, with a five-year band of 7.1. Inside this series, read it in its own company: it is the only rated French-language public secondary genuinely downtown, and its number reflects a full French-language program — including the provincial arts magnet and the board’s gifted centre — taught to every student in the sector, not a selected intake.

Use the rating the way it deserves to be used: as a measure of past academic results at the school, not a promise about any one child. What the number cannot show is the francophone environment itself, the arts studios your teenager will actually work in, or the walk through Lowertown every morning. Visit the school, walk the streets around it, and let the rating be one input — a fair one — among several.

De La Salle or the Other CEPEO Secondaries? The French Public Choice

Ottawa’s French public map has three secondaries in this series. East of the city, Gisèle-Lalonde serves Orléans from boulevard Millennium; in the south-west, Pierre-de-Blois serves Barrhaven and everything south and east of it toward Russell; and here downtown, De La Salle serves the core. The practical difference for a buyer is simple: one board, three French public high schools, and sector lines running between them. If the school is the reason for the move, the address check comes before the offer — every time.

There are also English-board and French Catholic mirrors to know downtown: the same central streets sit inside the Lisgar and Glebe stories for the Ottawa-Carleton board, and downtown’s French Catholic secondary is Saint-Jean-Baptiste, at LeBreton.
