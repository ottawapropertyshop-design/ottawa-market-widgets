The Short Answer

John McCrae Secondary School is an Ottawa-Carleton District School Board (OCDSB) secondary school at 103 Malvern Drive in Barrhaven, teaching Grades 9 to 12 in the English and French Immersion programs. The board publishes its boundary as its own entry — the John McCrae S.S. English and Immersion Grade 9 to 12 Attendance Boundary on its official secondary boundary index, updated January 10, 2025, drawn on the board’s map printed September 2024 — and answers for one exact address at a time through its School Locator, listed as up to date for the 2026–2027 school year. In the Fraser Institute’s 2025 secondary report card, John McCrae rates 7.2 out of 10, ranked #178 of 747 in Ontario. One boundary covers both programs: English and Immersion students draw from the same line.

The Catchment, From the Board’s Own Line

A catchment — the board calls it an attendance boundary — is the area a school draws its students from, and for John McCrae three things about the line matter to a buyer. First, the board decides, not the map: the school at the end of the street is not evidence, and proximity is a hint, not an answer — the locator result on the exact address is. Second, this school’s line is not one shape but two: a Barrhaven home can sit five minutes from the building and outside the line, while a Chapman Mills home across Woodroffe Avenue sits inside it. Third, lines move: boards review boundaries as neighbourhoods grow and enrolment shifts, which is why this series names the board source and its update date on every guide, and re-checks its catchments every September.

The option areas deserve a plain explanation, because listing write-ups blur them. Inside an option area, the board gives the family a choice between two named schools — John McCrae or South Carleton for the Half Moon Bay and Barrhaven South side, John McCrae or Longfields-Davidson Heights for the Longfields strip. A home there is not assigned to John McCrae; it is eligible to choose it, under the board’s option rules, and the choice is the family’s to make and the board’s to administer. That is a genuinely useful position for a buyer who wants the McCrae door open — and it is a different position from a designated address, which is why the listings below are drawn on the designated areas only. One distinction is worth stating plainly because buyers mix it up: transportation eligibility through OSTA, which runs home-to-school transportation for the OCDSB, is a distance test applied inside the boundary — it is not the boundary itself.

The Path Inward: Who Feeds John McCrae

The north area arrives through one middle school. Students in old Barrhaven move to Cedarview Middle School for Grades 7 and 8, then to John McCrae Secondary School for Grades 9 to 12. Cedarview’s published feeder list draws from Barrhaven Public School, Jockvale Elementary School, Mary Honeywell Elementary School, Half Moon Bay Elementary School and Wazoson Public School — so the elementary years differ street by street, but the path converges at Cedarview and lands at John McCrae. The Mary Honeywell path is the one Barrhaven families cite most: Mary Honeywell Elementary School to Cedarview Middle School to John McCrae.

The east area has its own elementary door. The southeast side of the line — the Chapman Mills and Rideau Heights streets east of Woodroffe Avenue — runs its elementary years through Half Moon Bay Public School: a Cambrian Road address checked on the board’s locator returns Half Moon Bay Public School (JK–6), then Cedarview Middle School (7–8), then John McCrae. Chapman Mills Public School itself, west of Woodroffe, sits outside this boundary — its catchment was redirected toward Longfields-Davidson Heights S.S. in a phase-in completed in 2023, so a Chapman Mills P.S. address is not a John McCrae path. What does not change is the designated secondary on the board’s current boundary map: the Fraser Fields and Sweetbriar streets east of Woodroffe Avenue sit inside John McCrae’s designated area for Grades 9 to 12, in both the English and Immersion programs.

Questions Families Ask First

Does buying a home inside the John McCrae catchment guarantee my child a place at the school?
No. School assignment is always the school board’s decision, boundaries can change, and programs fill. A home inside the line is a strong starting point for a search — never a guarantee. Confirm the schools assigned to the exact address with the board’s school locator, and directly with any other school you are considering, before you firm up an offer.

How do I check which school a Barrhaven address belongs to?
Use the Ottawa-Carleton District School Board’s official School Locator, which the board lists as up to date for the 2026–2027 school year. Enter the exact address — not just the street or the neighbourhood — because this boundary runs in two separate areas and along lot lines: two homes on the same street can sit on different sides of it.

What are the option areas on the John McCrae map?
Two areas on the board’s map are hatched as options rather than designated. In Half Moon Bay and Barrhaven South, families may choose John McCrae Secondary School or South Carleton High School; in the strip through Longfields, families may choose John McCrae or Longfields-Davidson Heights Secondary School. A home in an option area is eligible to choose John McCrae under the board’s option rules — it is not assigned there — which is why this guide’s listings are drawn on the designated areas only.

Do the English and French Immersion programs at John McCrae share one boundary?
Yes. The board publishes a single combined entry — John McCrae S.S. English and Immersion, Grades 9 to 12 — and one boundary map for both programs. The line on this page is that combined line, so it serves an English-program family and an Immersion family the same way.

Which schools feed into John McCrae?
For Grades 7 and 8, the north area of the catchment feeds through Cedarview Middle School, which draws from Barrhaven Public School, Jockvale Elementary School, Mary Honeywell Elementary School, Half Moon Bay Elementary School and Wazoson Public School. The southeast area feeds through Half Moon Bay Public School into the same Cedarview Middle School, then John McCrae — so both designated areas converge on one path from Grade 7 onward.

What does John McCrae’s Fraser rating of 7.2 actually measure?
It is the Fraser Institute’s report-card rating for the school — 7.2 out of 10, ranked #178 of 747 in the 2025 secondary report card. It is one input among many: it says nothing about whether a specific address falls inside the line, and it is not a promise about any individual child’s experience. This series prints the figure with its source and year so you can weigh it properly.

Are the listings on this page really inside the catchment?
Yes — that is the promise this page is built on. The searches run on the school’s own official board boundary, digitized from the board’s map, not on a postal code or a neighbourhood outline. Every listing address the searches display was checked against that line, and the numbers in each snapshot count exactly the homes its map displays — so when you click a home here, it sits inside one of John McCrae’s two designated areas. Confirm any single address on the board’s locator before you firm up an offer, the same check this guide runs.
