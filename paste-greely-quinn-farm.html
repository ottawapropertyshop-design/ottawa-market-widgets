The community — five streets in the heart of the village

Quinn Farm sits in the heart of Greely Village, on the Greely Loop — the village’s walking and cycling circuit — which puts it closer to the village core than most of Greely’s estate communities. The community is carried on five streets: Still Meadow Way, the longest street with 68 homes in the City’s address records, Rangeland Avenue with 26, Perennial Way with 19, Springrun Place with 7 and Stonecrop Avenue with 1 — 121 homes in all. The street names tell the design story: meadows, rangeland and perennial plantings — an eco-community planned around the rural character of the village it sits in.

Stonecrop Avenue is the community’s western edge and its connector. It runs south from Parkway Road into Still Meadow Way today; its southern piece, past Calwood Avenue and Calwood Park, is planned to join it as the green lands between Quinn Farm and Shadow Ridge build out. Those lands — and the corridor running west toward Cadieux Way — are Quinn Farm’s future phase, and the lots coming to market there are tracked in the land section of this guide.

Because lots are still releasing, Quinn Farm offers two different buys in one community: a new home built on a newly released lot, or a resale on one of the finished streets where the landscaping is already in. Either way, the setting is the same — a village-centre address, on the Loop, minutes on foot or by bike from the village’s daily services.

Living in Quinn Farm

Daily life here is the village-centre end of Greely: four quiet streets on the Greely Loop, with the village core about 1.6 km north — The Shoppes of Greely plaza on Bank Street for groceries and everyday services, the Greely Community Centre and the library branch on Meadow Drive. Bank Street (Ottawa Road 31) is the spine north: downtown Ottawa is roughly half an hour in good traffic. The honest transit picture matches the rest of Greely: no regular OC Transpo route runs through the village — the Thursday Shopper Route 304 is the one exception — so Quinn Farm is a driver’s address, and school buses, run by the Ottawa Student Transportation Authority, are the transit that matters to families here.
