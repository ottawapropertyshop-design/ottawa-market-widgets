The Rideau Forest story — how this Manotick estate pocket came to be

Most luxury neighbourhoods are built by builders. Rideau Forest was built by a land company and its buyers. Rideau Forest Developments Ltd. — the Ken Gordon Holdings family company, based at 5542 Ann Street in Manotick village — assembled the wooded Manotick land east of the river and sold it the slow way: serviced estate lots, released in phases over decades, one buyer at a time. Each Manotick buyer then built a custom home with their own builder and architect. The result is a community with no builder’s row and no repeated elevation — individual stone, stucco and brick houses, each designed for its own lot, inside one continuous forest.

Design control is the story. The Manotick vendor keeps it deliberately: working drawings go to the developer for written approval before a building permit is issued, homes are built to the approved plans, and lot clearing is limited to the approved building envelope — the trees outside the envelope stay. That is the whole secret of the place. A production builder protects a streetscape for a sales cycle; Rideau Forest’s design control has protected a forest for decades, which is why the developer’s own line about the Manotick community — where you can’t see the homes for the trees — is a description, not a slogan.

Rideau Forest Manotick questions, answered

What is Rideau Forest?
Rideau Forest is an estate enclave on the east bank of the Rideau River in Manotick East (OREB district 8005), inside the City of Ottawa. It is not a builder subdivision: the developer sells serviced estate lots in phases, and every buyer builds a custom home with their own builder. The streets — Rideau Forest Drive, Queenscourt Crescent, Blackhorse Court, Lords Manor Lane and their neighbours — run through mature oak, maple and pine, and the homes sit deep in the trees on lots of two acres and up. It is one of Manotick’s and Ottawa’s top luxury pockets, minutes from Manotick village and closer to Greely than to the village core.

How does buying in Rideau Forest work?
Two ways. Most Manotick buyers purchase a resale home — an existing custom estate that comes to market one at a time. The other way is the land play the community was built on: buy a serviced Manotick estate lot from the developer or on resale, then build a custom home with your own builder and architect. Either way, the developer’s design control applies to new builds: plans need the developer’s written approval before a building permit, and clearing is limited to the approved building envelope. That approval step is part of the purchase homework, not an afterthought.

Who is the developer behind Rideau Forest?
Rideau Forest Developments Ltd. — the Ken Gordon Holdings family company of Manotick, based at 5542 Ann Street in the Manotick village. The family company has sold the enclave’s serviced estate lots in phases over decades, keeping design control over what gets built: written plan approval before a permit, and lot clearing limited to the building envelope. That single-vendor, long-horizon model is why a community of individually designed stone, stucco and brick homes still reads as one composed landscape.

What services do Rideau Forest homes use?
Manotick estate-lot servicing: underground hydro, cable, internet and natural gas run to the lots, while each home runs on its own private well and septic system — not municipal water and sewer. On any purchase here, the well and septic homework is standard: a potability and bacteria water test, a flow check, and a septic inspection with the tank pumped and the bed assessed. Properties near regulated natural features can also involve Rideau Valley Conservation Authority review, which your lawyer and builder flag early in the process.

How big are the lots in Rideau Forest?
The Manotick community’s character is the two-acre class: lots of two acres and up, with the later phases’ zoning minimums set near that range. That is the honest way to read it — as the enclave’s standard and overwhelming pattern, not a surveyed guarantee for any single lot. A handful of older lots sit just under the line, so the exact acreage of the lot you are buying is always confirmed on its own survey and listing documents.

Why will there never be another Rideau Forest?
Because the planning rules that allowed it no longer exist. The City of Ottawa’s current Official Plan no longer permits new rural estate lot subdivisions. Rideau Forest’s remaining phases proceed only under grandfathering — the draft plan for Phases 7 to 11 was approved on August 25, 2006, under the former regional and township plans. The most recent phase on the public record, Phase 9 — part of 1120 Manotick Station Road, about 34 hectares and 43 lots, in the City’s September 2, 2021 committee report — moves under those old approvals. When the grandfathered phases are built out, the supply of new estate lots of this class, this close to Manotick and the city, ends with them.
